Card05Orbit & lids

Orbit, adnexa, and lids: the septum decides

The front of the eye is mostly benign lumps and lid position, with two things that must not be missed: an infection that has crossed the orbital septum, and a proptosis that means thyroid eye disease. Sort those first, then the rest.

01 Preseptal vs orbital cellulitis

One line, the orbital septum, separates a lid problem from a sight-and-life emergency. The globe signs tell you which side the infection is on.

Do not miss it

Orbital cellulitis spreads from the ethmoid sinus through the paper-thin lamina papyracea. Proptosis, painful eye movements, falling vision, or an RAPD means it is behind the septum: IV antibiotics, imaging, and possible drainage, before it becomes an abscess or a cavernous sinus thrombosis.

02 Proptosis means thyroid until proven otherwise

Thyroid eye disease (Graves)

The most common cause of proptosis in adults, unilateral or bilateral. Look for lid retraction and lag, proptosis, a restrictive myopathy (the muscle bellies enlarge, the tendons are spared), exposure keratopathy, and the sight-threatening one, compressive optic neuropathy. The muscles go in the order "I'M SLOW": Inferior rectus, Medial, Superior, Lateral. Smoking makes it worse.

Other proptosis to know

In a child with rapidly progressive proptosis, think rhabdomyosarcoma, an emergency. The most common benign adult orbital tumor is a cavernous hemangioma; a painful, steroid-responsive proptosis with no mass is idiopathic orbital inflammation (pseudotumor).

Lid cancer red flags

Madarosis (lash loss), ulceration, induration, and a pearly rolled border with telangiectasia mean basal cell carcinoma, the most common, favoring the lower lid and medial canthus. A recurrent chalazion in an older patient can be a sebaceous cell carcinoma in disguise, biopsy it.

04 Ptosis and the lacrimal sac

Ptosis, by cause

Aponeurotic (age, a high lid crease, the common one). Myogenic, think myasthenia if it is fatigable and variable with diplopia, or CPEO. Neurogenic: a CN III palsy gives ptosis with a down-and-out, dilated eye; Horner's gives a mild ptosis with a miotic pupil. Always check the pupil.

Dacryocystitis

A painful red swelling at the medial canthus over the lacrimal sac, with tearing and mucopurulent reflux, from a nasolacrimal duct obstruction. Warm compresses and oral antibiotics acutely; the definitive fix is a dacryocystorhinostomy.

07 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Preseptal vs orbital cellulitis? Orbital has proptosis, painful restricted movements, reduced vision, and RAPD; preseptal has none of these (lids only).
  2. Usual source of orbital cellulitis? Ethmoid sinusitis, through the lamina papyracea.
  3. Most common cause of adult proptosis? Thyroid eye disease (Graves).
  4. Thyroid eye disease signs? Lid retraction and lag, proptosis, restrictive myopathy, exposure keratopathy, compressive optic neuropathy.
  5. EOM order in thyroid eye disease? "I'M SLOW": Inferior rectus, Medial, Superior, Lateral (bellies enlarge, tendons spared).
  6. Chalazion vs hordeolum? Chalazion = chronic painless meibomian lipogranuloma; hordeolum = acute infection (internal meibomian, external Zeis/Moll).
  7. Entropion vs ectropion? Entropion turns in (lashes rub the cornea); ectropion turns out (epiphora, exposure).
  8. Eyelid cancer red flags and the common one? Madarosis, ulceration, induration, pearly border with telangiectasia; basal cell carcinoma (lower lid, medial canthus).
  9. Which lid cancer mimics a recurrent chalazion? Sebaceous cell carcinoma.
  10. Ptosis causes and one feature each? Aponeurotic (age, high crease); myogenic (myasthenia, fatigable); neurogenic (CN III down-and-out + dilated; Horner mild + miotic).
  11. Dacryocystitis location and cause? Painful swelling at the medial canthus over the sac, from nasolacrimal duct obstruction.
  12. Most common childhood orbital malignancy and its urgency? Rhabdomyosarcoma; rapidly progressive proptosis, an emergency.
