Card07Dx patterns

Ocular disease: when you see X, think Y

On the MCU preboard the disease facts were mostly there. What failed was matching the classic description to the name, especially inside big look-alike families: the retinopathies, the uveitis causes, the glaucomas. This card drills that one skill. Each row is a signature and the single word it points to. Learn to read the picture, not to recite the list.

01 The skill this card trains

Board disease stems are pattern prompts. They hand you two or three signs and ask for the name. If you study diseases as isolated fact-lists, you freeze when the family members look alike. The fix is to learn each disease by its one discriminating sign, the feature that no sibling shares.

How to use every table below

Cover the left column and read only the signature. Say the disease out loud before you look. The signature, not the disease name, is what the board gives you first.

The exact flips you made

You read CRVO as hypertensive, hypertensive as arteriosclerotic, arteriosclerotic as central serous, central serous as diabetic, and diabetic as hypertensive. They chain because you were matching on "vascular + hemorrhage" instead of the one signature word. Anchor each on its unique sign: veins tortuous = CRVO, pale + cherry-red = CRAO, microaneurysms = diabetic, copper wire = arteriosclerotic, young + macular serous = CSCR, bone spicules = RP.

04 Glaucoma, sorted two ways

Two questions place any glaucoma: is the angle open or closed, and is it primary (no other cause) or secondary (something did it)?

StemAnswer
aqueous reaches the angle at all times, no other diseasePOAG
posterior synechiae block flowSecondary angle closure
not a glaucoma surgery (the odd one out)Capsulectomy
does NOT cause congenital glaucomaDiabetes
Congenital glaucoma company

Axenfeld, Sturge-Weber (encephalofacial hemangioma), and aniridia all cause congenital glaucoma. Diabetes does not. Real glaucoma surgeries: goniotomy, trabeculectomy, fistulizing ops. Capsulectomy is a lens procedure.

Consistency check

You got Axenfeld right on one stem and missed it on another with the same description. The fix is the anchor phrase, not the stem wording: strands to Schwalbe line = Axenfeld, central corneal opacity + Descemet defect = Peters, no iris = aniridia.

06 Lens: anomalies, refraction, cataract

StemAnswer

Lens shape drives the error

A rounder or more curved lens (spherophakia, lenticonus, nuclear cataract) adds plus power, so it shifts toward myopia. A lens that is tilted or notched (coloboma) throws astigmatism.

Pilocarpine raising outflow is the same fact as your G1 glaucoma content: the ciliary muscle contracts and pulls the trabecular meshwork open. Answer it from the mechanism, not from a list.

09 Two answer-key flags

Likely mis-keyed on the preboard

"Second sight" is nuclear cataract producing lenticular (index) myopia. The form marked "lenticular astigmatism" as correct, which is almost certainly wrong. Learn it as index myopia and do not trust that item's key.

Old terminology to recognize, not adopt

"Compensated glaucoma" was keyed to POAG on this exam. It is dated wording; know it only so a stem using it does not throw you.

10 Rebuild it on paper

This is retrieval practice for pattern-matching. Read a signature, name the disease before you check.

1 · Retinopathy signatures blind

Write the seven retinopathies and the single discriminating sign for each. Then have someone read you a sign and name the disease in under five seconds.

2 · Fill the glaucoma 2x2

Draw the open/closed by primary/secondary grid and put one example in each cell. Add "not a glaucoma surgery" and "not a congenital-glaucoma cause."

3 · Uveitis causes from the sign

Match fibrin+young, iris nodules+granulomatous, and greasy KPs+protean to their causes. Say why each sign fits.

4 · Congenital anomaly anchors

Write the one phrase that names Axenfeld, Peters, and aniridia. No stems, just the anchor sign.

5 · Teach the corneal opacity ladder

Explain nebula vs macula vs leukoma by density, and give the lens-shape rule for spherophakia vs coloboma.

11 Spaced-repetition deck

Drop these into your FoqusLab review queue on the 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Tortuous dark veins, "blood and thunder"? Central retinal vein occlusion.
  2. Pale retina, cherry-red spot, thread-like arteries? Central retinal artery occlusion.
  3. Microaneurysms, dot-blot hemorrhages, neovascularization? Diabetic retinopathy.
  4. Copper/silver wiring, lipid in the artery wall? Arteriosclerotic retinopathy.
  5. Young patient, serous macular edema from an RPE leak? Central serous chorioretinopathy.
  6. Bone-spicule pigment, attenuated vessels, waxy disc? Retinitis pigmentosa.
  7. Fibrin in the AC, young patient (uveitis cause)? Rheumatoid disease.
  8. Greasy mutton-fat KPs, protean uveitis? Tuberculosis.
  9. Posterior synechiae cause which glaucoma? Secondary angle-closure.
  10. Angle open, no other disease? Primary open-angle glaucoma.
  11. Which does NOT cause congenital glaucoma? Diabetes (Axenfeld, Sturge-Weber, aniridia do).
  12. Iris strands to Schwalbe line? Axenfeld syndrome.
  13. Central corneal opacity with Descemet defect? Peters anomaly.
  14. Spherophakia vs lens coloboma refraction? Myopia; astigmatism.
  15. Contact-lens mishandling conjunctivitis? Giant papillary conjunctivitis.
  16. Totally opaque cornea? Leukoma (nebula < macula < leukoma).
