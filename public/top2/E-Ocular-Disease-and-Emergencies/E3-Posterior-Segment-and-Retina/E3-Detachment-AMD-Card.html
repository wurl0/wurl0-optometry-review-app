Card04Vitreous & macula

Vitreous, detachment, and AMD: where the field goes

The back of the eye tells you where it is failing by which part of vision drops out. A curtain means the retina is peeling; a central blur means the macula; a shrinking rim of vision means the periphery. Start with the aging vitreous, then follow the field.

01 The aging vitreous and PVD

The vitreous is a gel that liquefies with age (syneresis) and peels off the retina, a posterior vitreous detachment. Mostly harmless, but its tug can tear the retina.

What a PVD feels like

Floaters (musca volitantes) and photopsia (flashes from the traction), and sometimes a Weiss ring, a ring-shaped floater where the vitreous let go of the disc. The vitreous base at the ora serrata is its firmest attachment and a common tear site.

The red flag

A shower of new floaters, persistent flashes, or a curtain or shadow means the traction may have torn the retina. That earns a dilated exam now, because a tear can progress to a rhegmatogenous detachment.

02 Retinal detachment

The sensory retina lifts off the RPE, along the same plane the two optic-cup walls left behind. Three ways it happens.

TypeCauseBreak?
Rhegmatogenous (most common)A retinal break, then fluid under the retina (myopia, PVD, lattice, trauma)Yes
TractionalFibrovascular membranes pull it off (PDR, ROP, sickle cell)No
ExudativeSubretinal fluid with no break (tumor, VKH, CSCR, malignant hypertension); shifting fluidNo
Signs and the clock

Flashes and floaters, then a curtain across the field, an undulating elevated retina, and Shafer's sign (tobacco dust, pigment in the anterior vitreous). Macula-on versus macula-off decides urgency: a macula-on detachment is an emergency to reattach before the fovea lifts.

The macular pattern

Drusen are the hallmark, and AMD takes central vision while sparing the periphery, the opposite of glaucoma and RP. New distortion on an Amsler grid is the warning that dry has turned wet, refer for anti-VEGF.

04 Follow the field

Where vision drops out points straight to the disease. This ties the whole retina chapter together.

The rule

Central loss is a macular disease (AMD, macular hole, CSCR). Peripheral-first, shrinking to a tunnel is RP or late glaucoma. A curtain or sector is a retinal detachment. Where the field goes tells you the layer.

05 Two more macular lesions

Hole and pucker

A macular hole gives a central scotoma and a positive Watzke-Allen sign (a slit beam looks broken). An epiretinal membrane (macular pucker) gives metamorphopsia and a cellophane sheen on the macula.

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. PVD symptoms? Floaters (musca volitantes), photopsia (flashes), sometimes a Weiss ring.
  2. PVD red flags for a retinal tear? A shower of new floaters, persistent flashes, or a curtain; do a dilated exam now.
  3. Most common retinal detachment and its cause? Rhegmatogenous, from a retinal break (myopia, PVD, lattice, trauma).
  4. Tractional vs exudative detachment? Tractional = fibrovascular pull (PDR, ROP), no break; exudative = subretinal fluid, no break (tumor, VKH, CSCR), shifting fluid.
  5. Shafer's sign? Tobacco dust, pigment in the anterior vitreous, a marker of retinal detachment.
  6. Macula-on vs macula-off, why it matters? Macula-on is an emergency to reattach before the fovea lifts and central vision is lost.
  7. Dry AMD hallmark and advanced form? Drusen; geographic atrophy; slowed by AREDS vitamins.
  8. Wet AMD mechanism, symptom, treatment? Choroidal neovascularization; metamorphopsia (Amsler distortion); anti-VEGF injections.
  9. Which field does AMD take? Central, sparing the periphery.
  10. Central vs peripheral field loss, which diseases? Central: AMD, macular hole, CSCR. Peripheral / tunnel: RP, late glaucoma.
  11. A curtain or sector field defect suggests? Retinal detachment.
  12. A detachment separates which two layers? The neural (sensory) retina from the RPE.
