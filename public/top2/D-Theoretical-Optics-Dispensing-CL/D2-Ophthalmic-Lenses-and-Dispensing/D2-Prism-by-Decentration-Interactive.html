1. See the prism appear

The dashed vertical line is the eye's line of sight, fixed straight ahead. Move the optical center off it and the eye looks through a thicker part of the lens, which acts as a prism. The base always points to the thicker glass.

Temporal (toward ear)centeredNasal (toward nose)
Plus follows, minus opposes. The base goes the way you decenter a plus lens, the opposite way for a minus lens.

2. Why it works

  • A prism's base is its thick edge. Light bends toward the base; the image shifts toward the thin apex.
  • Plus lens is thick in the center, so its base sits at the optical center. Look off-center and the base points back toward the OC.
  • Minus lens is thick at the rim, so its base sits at the edge. Look off-center and the base points out to the nearest rim.
  • Prescribing: to get base in, decenter a plus lens in, or a minus lens out. Prentice gives the amount: Δ = c × F, c in cm.
