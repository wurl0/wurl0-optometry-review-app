Card02Prism & power

The four calculations the paper keeps asking

Most of the optics arithmetic is prism, Prentice, vertex, and thick-lens power. Each is one formula with one thing to remember about direction or sign. Get those four cold and a whole block of the exam is free.

01 Prism: deflection, deviation, direction

A prism diopter is 1 cm of image shift at 1 m. Light bends toward the base; the image shifts toward the apex.

Deviation of a thin prism

δ = (n − 1) × A. Each part: (n−1) is how strongly the material bends light, A is the apical angle. Crown glass n = 1.523, A = 5°: δ = 0.523 × 5 = 2.62°.

02 Prentice's rule

Prism from an off-center lens

Prism (Δ) = decentration (cm) × power (D). Every lens is a stack of prisms; look away from its optical center and you pick up prism. Rearranged, decentration = prism / power. Example: to get 4Δ from a −5.75 D lens, c = 4 / 5.75 = 0.70 cm = 6.96 mm.

Direction: plus follows, minus opposes

For a plus lens the base points the same way as the decentration (decenter the OC nasally, get base in). For a minus lens the base points the opposite way (decenter nasally, get base out).

03 Combining prisms

Perpendicular prisms add by Pythagoras

A horizontal and a vertical prism before one eye combine to a single oblique prism: R = √(H² + V²), pointing into the quadrant of the two bases. Example: 5Δ base up with 3.5Δ base out gives √(25 + 12.25) = 6.10Δ, up-and-out.

Same-direction prisms just add

Two prisms in the same meridian add or subtract arithmetically; only perpendicular ones need the hypotenuse.

04 Vertex distance compensation

Move a strong lens, its effective power changes

F₂ = F₁ / (1 − d × F₁), where d is how far you moved the lens (meters), toward the eye is positive. Matters at about ±4 D and above. Move a plus lens closer and its effective plus drops; move a minus lens closer and its minus drops too.

Worked example

−5.75 D at 13 mm, refit to 9 mm (4 mm closer): F₂ = −5.75 / (1 − 0.004 × −5.75) = −5.75 / 1.023 = −5.62 D (a bit less minus).

05 Thick-lens power

Nominal vs back vertex power

Nominal (thin-lens) power is just F₁ + F₂. But a real lens has thickness, so the useful number is the back vertex power: BVP = F₂ + F₁ / (1 − (t/n) × F₁). Here t is center thickness (m), n the index, and the fraction boosts the front power by the light's travel inside the lens.

Worked example

F₁ = +10.25, F₂ = −6.50, t = 8 mm, n = 1.67. t/n = 0.00479; front term = 10.25 / (1 − 0.00479 × 10.25) = 10.78; BVP = −6.50 + 10.78 = +4.28 D (nominal would read only +3.75).

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. One prism diopter? 1 cm of image displacement at 1 m.
  2. Thin-prism deviation? delta = (n - 1) x apical angle A.
  3. Light and image direction through a prism? Light bends toward the base; the image shifts toward the apex.
  4. Prentice's rule? Prism (prism diopters) = decentration (cm) x power (D).
  5. Decentration for a target prism? c (cm) = prism / power (e.g., 4 / 5.75 = 0.70 cm = 6.96 mm).
  6. Prism-base direction from decentration? Plus lens: base follows the decentration; minus lens: base opposes it.
  7. Combine perpendicular prisms? R = sqrt(H^2 + V^2), directed into the quadrant of the two bases.
  8. 5 up + 3.5 out resultant? 6.10 prism diopters, up-and-out.
  9. Vertex compensation formula and threshold? F2 = F1 / (1 - d F1); matters at about +/-4 D and above.
  10. Move a plus (or minus) lens closer, effect? Effective plus decreases; effective minus decreases (both drop).
  11. Back vertex power? BVP = F2 + F1 / (1 - (t/n) F1); nominal = F1 + F2.
  12. BVP of +10.25 / -6.50, t 8 mm, n 1.67? About +4.28 D.
