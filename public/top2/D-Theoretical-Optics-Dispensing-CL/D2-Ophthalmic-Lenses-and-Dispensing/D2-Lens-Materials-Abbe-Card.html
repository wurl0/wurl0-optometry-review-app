Card04Materials

Two numbers pick the lens: index and Abbe

A material is chosen by how thin it makes the lens (index) and how faithfully it passes color (Abbe), and the two usually fight. Add impact resistance, coatings, and tints, and you can answer every material item by placing it on one trade-off.

01 Index vs Abbe, the trade-off

Refractive index sets thinness (higher index bends light more, so flatter, thinner lenses). Abbe number sets color fidelity (higher Abbe = less chromatic dispersion). Push the index up and the Abbe usually falls.

03 Chromatic aberration and impact

Low Abbe means colored fringes

A low-Abbe material disperses more, so a patient looking through the edge of a high-index or polycarbonate lens sees colored fringes around lights, even with sharp central acuity. Polycarbonate (Abbe 30) is the worst; crown/CR-39 (about 58) the best.

When safety wins

Polycarbonate and Trivex are the impact-resistant choices, for children, monocular patients, sports, and industry. Trivex is the upgrade: poly-like safety but a higher Abbe (crisper optics) and lower density. Impact is checked by the drop-ball test.

05 Absorptive lenses and tints

Pick the tint for the task

Yellow/amber: blocks blue, boosts contrast in fog, haze, and low light. Gray: neutral, true color, general sun. Brown: contrast in bright sun. Polaroid: kills glare from horizontal reflected surfaces (water, road).

Two glass facts

Glass is tinted in the melt: cobalt oxide gives a blue lens. And a glassblower's cataract comes from chronic infrared exposure, the reason heat workers wear IR-absorbing lenses.

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. What do index and Abbe each control? Index = thinness (higher = thinner); Abbe = color fidelity (higher = less dispersion).
  2. The trade-off? Higher index usually means lower Abbe (thinner but more chromatic aberration).
  3. Highest Abbe materials? Crown glass and CR-39 (about 58 to 59).
  4. Lowest Abbe / most fringing? Polycarbonate (about 30).
  5. Impact-resistant materials? Polycarbonate and Trivex.
  6. Trivex advantage over polycarbonate? Higher Abbe (crisper optics) with excellent impact resistance and lower density.
  7. Why does high-index need AR? Higher index reflects more light at each surface (Fresnel).
  8. Plastic vs glass and UV? Plastic (CR-39) absorbs more UV than crown glass.
  9. Highest-index common plastic? 1.74 (thinnest).
  10. Colored fringes at the lens edge mean? Chromatic aberration from a low Abbe value.
  11. Yellow tint use? Blocks blue, boosts contrast in fog/haze and low light.
  12. Cobalt oxide and glassblower cataract? Cobalt = blue glass; glassblower cataract = infrared exposure.
