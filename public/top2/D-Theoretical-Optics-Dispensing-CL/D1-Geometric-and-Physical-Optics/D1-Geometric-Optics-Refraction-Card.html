Card01Refraction

How light bends, and where it focuses

Every optics computation on the paper is one of a few ideas: light slows in denser media and bends, it disperses by wavelength, and a lens adds vergence. Learn Snell, the critical angle, the vergence equation, and the sign rules, and the calculation items become plug-and-play.

01 Index and Snell's law

The refractive index is how much a medium slows light: n = c / v. Entering a denser (higher-n) medium, light slows and bends toward the normal.

What changes at the boundary, and what does not

Crossing into glass, speed and wavelength drop, but frequency stays the same (frequency is set by the source, so color is conserved). Snell's law is n1 sinθ1 = n2 sinθ2, the angle depends only on the two indices.

02 Critical angle and total internal reflection

Going the other way (dense to rare)

Leaving a dense medium, the ray bends away from the normal. Past the critical angle it cannot leave at all and reflects completely, total internal reflection. sinθc = n2 / n1, so a higher index gives a smaller critical angle.

Where you see it

Fiber-optic instrument cables and the sparkle of a diamond (very high index, tiny critical angle, so light bounces around inside before escaping).

03 Dispersion and color

A prism spreads white light

Index varies slightly with wavelength, so each color bends by a different amount, dispersion. Blue/violet bends more (shorter wavelength, higher frequency) and focuses closer (shorter focal length) than red, the basis of longitudinal chromatic aberration.

Order and the Abbe preview

By increasing frequency: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet (violet is highest frequency, shortest wavelength). A material's Abbe number rates how little it disperses, high Abbe = little color fringing (its own card ahead).

04 Vergence and power

The one equation that runs the eye and every lens

L' = L + F: outgoing vergence = incoming vergence + lens power. Vergence (in diopters) = n divided by the distance in meters; converging light is plus, diverging is minus. Lens power F = 1 / f (focal length in meters), so a stronger lens has a shorter focal length.

Worked example

A diverging lens of −8.50 D has focal length f = 1 / (−8.50) = −0.118 m (negative, virtual focus on the incoming side).

Magnification sign

m = image distance / object distance (or L / L'). Negative m = inverted and real; positive m = upright and virtual. A diverging lens is always positive m and less than 1.

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Refractive index? n = c / v, the ratio of light speed in vacuum to its speed in the medium.
  2. Which way does light bend into a denser medium? Toward the normal (it slows down).
  3. What is conserved crossing a boundary? Frequency (color); speed and wavelength change.
  4. Snell's law? n1 sin(theta1) = n2 sin(theta2); the angle depends on the two indices.
  5. Critical angle and TIR? sin(theta c) = n2/n1; past it, light totally internally reflects (dense to rare only).
  6. Higher index does what to the critical angle? Makes it smaller (easier TIR), as in a diamond.
  7. Which color disperses/bends most and focuses closest? Blue/violet (shorter wavelength, shorter focal length).
  8. Increasing-frequency color order? Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet.
  9. Power and focal length? F = 1/f (meters); stronger lens = shorter focal length.
  10. The vergence equation? L' = L + F (outgoing vergence = incoming + lens power).
  11. Focal length of a -8.50 D lens? -0.118 m (virtual focus).
  12. Diverging lens image? Always virtual, upright, minified (positive m, less than 1).
