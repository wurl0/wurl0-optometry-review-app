Card03Photometry

Light falls off by the square, reflects by the index

Two ideas run the rest of the arithmetic: brightness drops with the square of distance, and every surface reflects a slice set by its index. Add transmission and optical density and you can answer every illumination and tint item without hunting for a formula.

01 The inverse-square law

Illuminance (lux or foot-candles) from a point source falls with the square of distance: E = I / d², where I is luminous intensity (candela) and d the distance.

Two worked items

3000 cd at 35 ft: E = 3000 / 35² = 3000/1225 = 2.45 fc. Photometer match (equal illuminance, I₁/d₁² = I₂/d₂²): an unknown lamp at 15 m equals 105 cd at 9 m, so I = 105 × (15²/9²) = 291.67 cd.

02 Shadows: umbra and penumbra

Two zones behind an object

The umbra is the full shadow (the source is entirely blocked); the penumbra is the partial ring (part of the source still peeks through). Only a true point source gives a pure umbra with no penumbra.

Which way the umbra goes

If the object is smaller than the source, the umbra narrows to a point and ends. If the object is larger than the source, the umbra widens with distance and never closes.

03 Reflection at a surface (Fresnel)

A slice reflects at every air-lens boundary

R = ((n − 1)/(n + 1))² per surface. Each part: the mismatch (n−1) over the sum (n+1), squared. Crown glass n = 1.523: R = (0.523/2.523)² = 4.30%. A 1.70 lens reflects (0.70/2.70)² = 6.72%, higher index reflects more, which is why high-index lenses need AR coating.

04 Transmission and series systems

Through both surfaces, then through everything in the path

An uncoated lens transmits T = (1 − R)² (two surfaces). CR-39 (R = 3.87% each): T = 0.9613² = 92.41%. Systems in series multiply: a lens at 80% behind a windshield at 80% passes 0.80 × 0.80 = 64%. Opacity = 1 − T, so 64% transmission is 36% opacity.

05 Optical density (tints)

A log scale that adds

OD = log₁₀(1 / T). A tint at 50% transmission has OD = log(2) = 0.30; the combined 64% system has OD = log(1/0.64) = 0.19. Because it is a log, densities add when you stack filters, while transmissions multiply.

06 Cross-links

Say these out loud

Card 1: reflection and index sit on the same n that drives refraction. Lens materials card ahead: Fresnel reflection is exactly why high-index needs AR, and tints/optical density belong to absorptive lenses. Clinical: illumination and glare drive low-vision lighting and photophobia tints.

  6. Fresnel reflection per surface? R = ((n-1)/(n+1))^2.
  7. Crown glass single-surface reflection? About 4.3% (n = 1.523).

  9. Transmission through two surfaces? T = (1 - R)^2 (uncoated).
