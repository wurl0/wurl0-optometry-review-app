Card06Contact lenses

Three ways to sit on the eye: drape, rest, vault

A contact lens either drapes over the cornea (soft), rests on it (corneal RGP), or vaults it with a tear lake (scleral). That choice, plus base curve and oxygen, decides the fit; a short list of complications decides the safety. Learn both and the contact-lens items are recognition.

02 Fitting parameters

The numbers

Base curve is chosen from the corneal curvature (keratometry). Diameter and power complete a soft lens. Oxygen is Dk (a material property) and Dk/t (transmissibility, so a thinner lens breathes more). Silicone hydrogel raised soft-lens Dk sharply.

Special fits

Keratoconus: scleral (or RGP) to vault the cone. Aphakia: RGP for the high-plus optics. Ortho-k: a reverse-geometry RGP worn overnight that temporarily reshapes the cornea (and imposes myopic defocus for control).

The two organisms to name

Pseudomonas aeruginosa is the most common contact-lens keratitis bug. Acanthamoeba comes from water (swimming, tap-water rinsing), a ring infiltrate with severe pain, and hydrogen peroxide is the care system that best resists it.

05 Cross-links

Say these out loud

Clinical Optometry: the same CL fitting, keratoconus, and complications appear there. Primary Care (Myopia control): ortho-k and dual-focus lenses. Ocular Disease: microbial keratitis, GPC, and neovascularization are the disease side of these complications.

07 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. How do soft, corneal RGP, and scleral sit? Soft drapes the cornea, corneal RGP rests on it, scleral vaults it with a tear reservoir.
  2. Base curve is chosen from? The corneal curvature (keratometry).
  3. Dk vs Dk/t? Dk = material oxygen permeability; Dk/t = transmissibility (thinner lens = more oxygen).
  4. Best lens for keratoconus? Scleral (or RGP) to vault the cone.
  5. Best lens for aphakia? RGP (crisp high-plus optics).
  6. What is ortho-k? An overnight reverse-geometry RGP that temporarily reshapes the cornea (and slows myopia).
  7. Most common CL keratitis organism? Pseudomonas aeruginosa.
  8. Acanthamoeba source and best care system? Water exposure; hydrogen peroxide best resists it.
  9. Corneal neovascularization means? Chronic hypoxia; raise Dk/t and reduce wear.
  10. GPC signs? Upper-lid papillae, itch, and mucous discharge; stop the lens, move to dailies.
  11. CL corneal ulcer management? Stop lens wear and start broad-spectrum antibiotics; never a steroid first.
  12. Advantage of daily disposables? No care needed; best hygiene and convenience.
