Card07Optics & CL fixes

Optics and contact lenses: pick the lens, run the number

Two families carried most of the misses across the Theoretical Optics and the Clinical papers: choosing the right contact lens for a given cornea and problem, and a handful of dispensing calculations and material facts. The contact-lens items from both papers are gathered here, since contact lenses live in this subject.

01 Which contact lens for which problem

Match the cornea and the complaint to the lens. Rigid neutralizes irregularity; soft is for comfort and regular corneas.

PatientBest lens
keratoconus / irregular cornea after traumascleral (or corneal RGP)
dry-eye patient who wants contact lensesdaily disposable silicone hydrogel
aphakia (high plus)soft spherical lens
high myopia with astigmatismtoric soft lens
presbyopia wanting extended depth of focusaspheric soft multifocal
Why soft fails keratoconus

A soft lens drapes over the cone and takes its shape, so it cannot neutralize irregular astigmatism. A rigid lens holds its own front surface and creates a smooth tear lens over the irregular cornea, which is what corrects the vision.

The red-flag complication

Sudden pain, photophobia, and decreased vision in a lens wearer, or a ring-shaped corneal infiltrate with hypopyon after extended wear, is infectious (microbial) keratitis. Stop lens wear and start broad-spectrum antibiotics. Red, itchy eyes with mucous discharge is instead giant papillary conjunctivitis.

Abbe and jump

Higher Abbe means less chromatic aberration; among common materials crown glass and CR-39 sit high, high-index plastics low. Image jump comes from the segment's optical center: a flat-top puts it at the top of the segment, minimizing jump; a round segment maximizes it.
