The practical exam: perform it, then say it

You draw a lot, get one item to perform plus five written questions, and an examiner who watches and asks. Every station rewards the same two skills: do the procedure clean, and say the why behind it. This is the phone-sized digest of your clinic scripts. The printable long version lives in your notes.

00 The one habit that carries every room

Universal intro skeleton

"Good morning, I'm [name]. I'll be performing [test]. This checks [what]. It's non-invasive and won't hurt. Please sit, chin here, forehead here. Look at the target and tell me what you see. Any questions?"

Three reflexes graders reward

1. Infection control first — hands and instrument before you touch anything. 2. Talk while you work — silence reads as guessing. 3. Draw a lot, then breathe — say the intro to buy recall time, then move; motion unlocks the steps.

Phoria vs tropia, the answer to half of Room 1

A phoria is a hidden drift that only shows when you break fusion (cover an eye). A tropia is a turn visible with both eyes open. Eso = in, exo = out, hyper = higher, hypo = lower.

Tool selection is graded

The bench lays out a table of instruments. Part of many stations is picking the correct tool for the drawn test, then naming its use and showing how. Know what you reach for, not just the steps (NPC → RAF rule or an accommodative target, not a random penlight). The Tool table below maps every test; Low vision is its own pick-and-explain drill.

01 Room 1 · Binocular Vision

Theme: is the eye straight, and do the two eyes team? Nearly every question reduces to phoria vs tropia and its direction.

1.1Cover Test

"I'll cover and uncover each eye. Keep this letter clear and single."

  1. Fixate a target at 40 cm and 6 m, one line above threshold.
  2. Cover-uncover (finds tropia): cover one eye, watch the other. Refixation movement = tropia; out-move = eso, in-move = exo.
  3. Alternating (total deviation): swing the occluder side to side, watch each eye as uncovered.
They ask
  • Cover-uncover finds tropia (watch uncovered eye); alternating gives the total but breaks fusion so it can't split phoria from tropia.
  • Out-movement = eso, in-movement = exo.

1.2Prism Cover Test (PCT)

"I'll hold a prism while I cover and uncover; tell me if the target still shifts."

  1. Find the direction by alternating cover first.
  2. Prism apex toward the deviation: eso = base-out, exo = base-in, hyper = base-down on the high eye.
  3. Increase power until the refixation neutralises. That value = the angle in Δ.
They ask
  • Esotropia needs base-out. Unit is the prism dioptre (1Δ = 1 cm shift at 1 m).

1.3 / 1.4Hirschberg & Krimsky

"I'll shine a light; look straight at it."

  1. Penlight at 50 cm; read the corneal reflex position. 1 mm decentration ≈ 7° ≈ 15Δ.
  2. Reflex temporal = eso; nasal = exo (Hirschberg, gross estimate).
  3. Krimsky: place prism over the fixating eye until the reflex in the deviated eye centres = the angle.
They ask
  • 1 mm ≈ 7°. Reflex temporal means the eye is turned in (eso).
  • Use Krimsky when the patient can't fixate or has poor vision in one eye.

1.5Ocular Motility / EOM

"Follow the light with your eyes only, head still. Tell me if it doubles or hurts."

  1. Broad H through the 9 gazes; watch both eyes (versions).
  2. If a muscle underacts, cover the other eye and test that eye alone (duction).
They ask
  • Up-and-right = right superior rectus + left inferior oblique (yoke pair).
  • Version = both eyes; duction = one eye. LR6 (CN VI), SO4 (CN IV), rest CN III.

1.6 / 1.7NPC & NPA

"Keep it single (NPC) / clear (NPA) as I move it in. Tell me the moment it splits / blurs."

  1. NPC: move a target to the nose; record break then recovery. Normal ≈ 5 cm or better.
  2. NPA: push a near card in to first sustained blur. Amplitude (D) = 100 / distance in cm.
They ask
  • Receded NPC (>10 cm) suggests convergence insufficiency.
  • Hofstetter minimum amp = 15 − 0.25(age); blur at 10 cm = 10 D.

1.8 / 1.9Maddox Rod & Maddox Wing

"Through this lens your eye sees a red line. Tell me where it sits versus the light."

  1. Red rod turns a spotlight into a streak so different the eyes dissociate, revealing a phoria.
  2. Rods horizontal → vertical streak → tests lateral phoria. Streak on the covered-eye side = exo; opposite = eso.
  3. Maddox Wing = near (33 cm), septum-dissociated, read the number the arrow points to directly.
They ask
  • Rod horizontal gives a vertical streak. Streak through the light = orthophoria.
  • It measures a phoria because the rod breaks fusion.

1.10Worth 4-Dot

"Wear the red-green glasses. How many lit dots, and what colours?"

  1. Red over right, green over left; 4 dots at 40 cm and 6 m.
  2. 4 = fusion. 2 red = left suppressed. 3 green = right suppressed. 5 = diplopia.
They ask
  • Five dots = diplopia, no suppression. The white dot takes the dominant eye's colour.
  • Near tests a larger retinal area; distance isolates the macula and reveals suppression.

1.11 - 1.13Stereo Fly · Brock String · RAF Rule

  1. Stereo (Titmus): polarised glasses, booklet flat at 40 cm; fly ≈3000″, circles graded to 40″. Smaller arc = better; contour stereo (has monocular cues).
  2. Brock string: to the nose; normal = two strings crossing exactly at the fixated bead. One string gone = suppression; cross in front = over-convergence.
  3. RAF rule: rests on the cheeks; measures NPC and NPA (amplitude) off its cm and D scales.

1.14 - 1.18Howell · Bagolini · Pencil · Flipper · Aperture

  1. Howell: vertical prism dissociates; arrow reads near lateral phoria off a colour scale.
  2. Bagolini striated: least-dissociating; a full centred X = normal correspondence + fusion, a gap in one streak = suppression scotoma.
  3. Two-pencil: tip-to-tip both eyes open = gross stereopsis and eye-hand coordination.
  4. Flipper ±2.00: facility in cycles/min (~8 cpm binocular). Slow on plus = can't relax; slow on minus = can't stimulate.
  5. Aperture rule: single aperture = convergence (BO), double = divergence (BI); control features prove real fusion.
21-point (OEP) if drawn

#7 = distance subjective refraction. NRA = add plus to blur (relax, expect +2.00); PRA = add minus to blur (stimulate, expect −2.37). Developed by the Optometric Extension Program.

02 Room 2 · Refraction & Phorometry

03 Room 3 · Instruments

Set up correctly, state what it measures, take a clean reading. Focus the eyepiece first on any scope, it zeroes out your own accommodation.

3.1Keratometry

"I'll measure the curvature of the front of your cornea. Non-invasive, won't touch your eye. Chin on the rest, look at the target."

  1. Focus the eyepiece first. Align the reticle on the mires; focus until crisp.
  2. Superimpose the plus mires (horizontal meridian), then the minus (vertical); read power and axis for each.
They ask
  • Focus the eyepiece first to remove examiner accommodation (else falsely steep).
  • Difference between the two readings = corneal astigmatism. WTR = steeper vertical.

3.2Lensometer

  1. Focus the eyepiece; lens back surface against the stop (back vertex power).
  2. First clear set of lines = sphere meridian (+ its axis); second = other meridian. Difference = cyl.
  3. Mark the optical centre; prism = mire displacement (each ring 1Δ). Add = extra plus in the near segment.
They ask
  • Back surface faces the stop. In minus-cyl form the most-plus meridian = sphere, its lines' axis = cyl axis.

3.3Slit Lamp

  1. Illumination arm 30-60°; start low mag, sweep lids → conjunctiva → cornea → AC → iris → lens.
  2. Know the illuminations: diffuse, direct/parallelepiped, optic section, retroillumination, sclerotic scatter, specular, Van Herick.
They ask
  • Van Herick = 60°, optic section at limbus, grade the AC angle. Specular reflection = endothelium. Optic section = corneal depth.

3.4 / 3.5Synoptophore & Schematic Eye

  1. Synoptophore: history, then objective angle (no eye movement) vs subjective angle (patient superimposes). Difference = angle of anomaly (ARC). Grades: simultaneous perception → fusion → stereopsis.
  2. Schematic eye: six cardinal points (2 principal, 2 nodal, 2 focal); Gullstrand total ~+60 D (cornea +43, lens +19). Reduced eye = one surface, ~+60 D.

04 Room 4 · Contact Lens Fitting

Measure the eye, pick a lens, prove the fit with fluorescein and lens movement.

4.1RGP Fitting

  1. Measure pupil size, HVID, K-readings, VA/refraction.
  2. Base curve: fit spheres on flat K; diameter ~ HVID − 2 mm.
  3. Insert trial lens, settle, add fluorescein, view under cobalt blue + Wratten.
  4. Blink-and-look-up: fast drop = low-riding (flat/heavy); sticks high = high-riding (steep/tight). Target 1-2 mm movement.
They ask
  • Steep = central pooling (apical clearance). Flat = central touch, peripheral pooling.
  • "Fit on K" = base curve equals flattest K. Fluorescein glows green showing tear-film thickness.

4.1bThe measurements and the math

They hand you K's, HVID and an Rx and ask for the trial lens. Drill this.

r = 337.5 / D  ·  D = 337.5 / r
ComputeRGPSCL
Diameter (TD)HVID − 2HVID + 2 (or +2.5 high water)
Base curve (from flatter K)337.5 / D337.5 / D + 0.7

Corneal astig = flatter K − steeper K (minus cyl, record horizontal first). WTR ~180, ATR ~90. Round BC to available (~0.3 mm; 8.4/8.6), within ±1 mm of flatter K. Higher BC = flatter.

Worked (from your review)

OU −3.00 −0.50×180, HVID 11, K 45.00@180 / 45.50@90 → TD = 11−2 = 9.0 mm; BOZR = 337.5/45.00 = 7.5 mm; power −3.00 (nearest available). Rides flat → drop to 7.4, recheck after 30 min.

They ask
  • RGP BC = 337.5 / flatter-K (fit on K); SCL = same + 0.7.
  • Flat = central touch + edge clearance; steep = central pooling; optimum = edge slightly lifted, even spread.
  • ↑Water = ↑Dk/O₂ but ↑index & ↑protein; thin = more O₂.

4.2 / 4.3Toric & Multifocal

  1. Toric: stabilised by prism ballast / thin zones / truncation. Ordering axis by rotation: LARS (Left Add, Right Subtract).
  2. Multifocal: simultaneous (both images on retina, brain selects) vs alternating/translating (lens shifts on down-gaze). Monovision = distance in dominant eye.

05 Room 5 · Ophthalmic Dispensing & Edging

Measure patient and frame, do the decentration maths, run the lab workflow in order. The maths is the most-asked part, so know it cold.

Frame PD = ES + DBL
Decentration / eye = (Frame PD − Patient PD) ÷ 2
Why these forms (formula depth)

ES = one lens-box width; DBL = the bridge gap. Each box centre sits half a lens-width from the bridge, so centre-to-centre = one full ES (two halves) + DBL. That is why it is ES + DBL.

If the frame is wider than the face, each OC starts too far out by the whole difference; split it between two eyes, hence ÷2. Frame PD > patient PD → decentre in; smaller → out.

Worked (from the review)

Plastic: ES 51 + 1 = 52, DBL 20 − 1 = 19 → Frame PD 71. Patient PD 66 → (71 − 66)/2 = 2.5 mm IN per eye. (Metal ES is inner-to-outer; plastic uses inner-to-inner + 1, DBL − 1.)

5.3Edging workflow (say it in order)

  1. Former making (dot the OC, draw 180 + fitting cross)
  2. Set cyl axis (SV) / mark fitting cross (PAL) / set segment height (bifocal)
  3. Mark fitting cross (SV) / set pupil height (PAL)
  4. Tracing / cutting
  5. Chipping / crumbing
  6. Edging (grind the bevel)
  7. Tinting
  8. Glazing / mounting, then verify OC and axis on the lensmeter
They ask
  • First step = former making / lens marking. Tracing before edging; tinting before glazing.
  • PAL fitting cross must sit at the pupil centre. Seg height measured to the lower lid margin.

Numbers they may ask

Equivalent magnification: M = F / 4 (25 cm reference; +20 D ≈ 5x). The 4 is the 25 cm baseline (1 / 0.25 m = 4 D).

Kestenbaum's rule (starting reading add): add in D ≈ reciprocal of the distance VA fraction. VA 20/200 → add ≈ 200/20 = +10.00 D; reading distance = 1 / add.

Telescope: M = Fo / Fe (objective over eyepiece power).

They ask

Galilean vs Keplerian? Galilean = upright, light, lower mag/field; Keplerian = inverted (needs prisms), higher mag/field. Stand vs hand magnifier? Stand for tremor/steadiness, hand for portability. Reading add estimate? Kestenbaum's rule. Why reverse-contrast on a CCTV? Cuts glare, helps many retinal/media conditions.

08 Rebuild it on paper

Reading this is not practising it. These pull the exam skill, not the recognition.

1 · Blind intro drill

Pick five random tests. Say the full intro skeleton for each from memory, swapping only the test name and the "checks what" clause. Aim for under 15 seconds each.

2 · Cover-test direction tree

Draw the eso and exo cases. Write which eye you watch, which way it moves, and the prism base to neutralise. Three clean rounds.

3 · VT#7 from zero

List steps 1 to 11 blind, then add the one-line "why" for fog, duochrome, JCC red dot, and when not to balance.

4 · Decentration on paper

Given ES, DBL, and patient PD, compute frame PD and decentration in or out. Do three sets (one metal, two plastic) without the formula in view.

5 · Blind tool pick

Lay every instrument you can find on a table. Have a partner call a random test; you grab the right tool in under 3 seconds and say its name, use, and first step. Repeat for the low-vision devices: pick, name, who it is for, how to use.

6 · Clinic run-through

At the La Union clinic: perform each drawn item talking the whole time, timed under 8 minutes, with a partner firing the "They ask" lines as the examiner.

09 Recall deck

Drop these into your FoqusLab queue. Answer out loud before you flip. These are the five-question and examiner spot-checks in miniature.

  1. Phoria vs tropia? Phoria = latent (needs fusion broken); tropia = manifest with both eyes open.
  2. Cover-uncover: eye moves out to fixate? It was turned in → esotropia.
  3. Prism base to neutralise an esotropia? Base-out.
  4. Hirschberg: 1 mm of reflex decentration equals? ~7 degrees ~15Δ; temporal reflex = eso.
  5. Krimsky prism goes over which eye? The fixating (good) eye.
  6. Amplitude of accommodation formula? 100 / near point in cm (blur at 10 cm = 10 D).
  7. Maddox rod horizontal gives what streak, testing what? Vertical streak; lateral phoria.
  8. Worth 4-dot: sees 5 dots? Diplopia, no suppression.
  9. Why always plus in fogging? To relax accommodation and avoid over-minus.
  10. Duochrome principle, and clearer on red means? Transverse chromatic aberration; add minus.
  11. Darker line on the clock dial is which meridian? The power meridian.
  12. JCC red dot represents? Minus cylinder axis (white = plus).
  13. When do you NOT do binocular balance? Amblyopia, suppression, one eye, pathology, presbyopia.
  14. Why focus the lensmeter/keratometer eyepiece first? To zero out examiner accommodation (else falsely steep / wrong sphere).
  15. RGP steep vs flat fluorescein pattern? Steep = central pooling; flat = central touch with peripheral pooling.
  16. Low-riding vs high-riding RGP cause? Low = flat/heavy (drops fast); high = steep/tight (sticks up).
  17. Toric CL: rotation compensation rule? LARS (Left Add, Right Subtract).
  18. Frame PD and decentration formulas? Frame PD = ES + DBL; decentration/eye = (frame PD − patient PD)/2.
  19. First step of edging, and tint vs glaze order? Former making; tint before glazing.
  20. Synoptophore: objective vs subjective angle differ means? Anomalous retinal correspondence; the gap is the angle of anomaly.
  21. Which tool do you pick for NPC and NPA? RAF rule (or an accommodative target; NPA can also use a near card + occluder).
  22. Galilean vs Keplerian telescope? Galilean = upright, lighter, lower mag/field; Keplerian = inverted (needs prisms), higher mag/field.
  23. Equivalent magnification of a +20 D magnifier? M = F/4 = 5x (25 cm reference).
  24. Kestenbaum's rule for a 20/200 reader? Add ≈ 200/20 = +10.00 D; reading distance = 1/add = 10 cm.
  25. Lateral phoria: measuring vs dissociating prism, and endpoint? OD 12-15Δ BI measures, OS 6Δ BU dissociates; endpoint vertical alignment (BI=exo, BO=eso).
  26. Convergence at far (#10) and abduction at far (#11) normals? #10 = 19/10 (BO); #11 = 9/5 (BI).
  27. PRC vs NRC at near normals? PRC (#16A, BO blur) = 15; NRC (#17A, BI blur) = 14.
  28. Expected PRA and NRA? PRA (minus to blur) = −2.50; NRA (plus to blur) = +2.00.
  29. RGP base curve and diameter from K and HVID? BC = 337.5 / flatter-K power; TD = HVID − 2 mm.
  30. Radius of curvature from a 45.00 D cornea? r = 337.5 / 45.00 = 7.5 mm.
