Subjective refraction & OEP: the step strategy

You do not memorise 28 disconnected steps. You learn two mental models and hang everything on them. Subjective refraction is one loop repeated per eye. OEP phorometry is one number you draw, and it is always one of four shapes. This card walks each step with a picture, the move, and the trap, then arms you for the examiner's five questions.

00 The two mental models

Model 1 — Subjective refraction = "fog, then peel to the retina"

Plain version: you deliberately make the eye a little short-sighted with plus lenses so it stops trying to focus (accommodation relaxes). Then you peel the plus away slowly until the sharpest image sits exactly on the retina at 20/20. Everything plus-heavy is on purpose, so you never accidentally hand out too much minus.

The clinical term for that relaxed-and-parked state: the far point is placed at optical infinity with accommodation relaxed.

Model 2 — OEP = "draw a number, name its shape"

The 21-point exam feels huge until you see that every test you could draw is one of four shapes: a lateral phoria, a vertical phoria, a horizontal vergence, or an accommodation test. Name the shape and the prisms, endpoint, and recording fall out automatically. You are not memorising 21 things, you are memorising four patterns and a table of normal values.

The 4-step spine of the refraction (say it as one breath)

Best sphere → cylinder → sphere refine → balance. Fog and defog find the sphere; the astigmatic dial and JCC find the cylinder; the duochrome refines the sphere; then you balance the two eyes. Four beats, repeated for each eye.

01 Subjective refraction, step by step

Card facts to say up front: distance 6 m / 20 ft, target 20/20 Snellen, lenses in place = VT#4 gross (retinoscopy) or the autorefractor result, illumination 20 ft-candle, endpoint = maximum plus to 20/20. Start OD, occlude OS.

1Fog (find best sphere, part 1)

Picture: pile plus in front of the eye until the biggest letter goes fully blurry. You have pushed the image well in front of the retina. The eye gives up focusing.

Do: add plus lenses until the largest letter is totally blurred.

Trap: not fogging enough. If you never fully blur, accommodation is still active and every later step drifts toward over-minus.

2Unfog / defog (best sphere, part 2)

Picture: peel the plus back one click at a time; the image walks backward toward the retina and the letters sharpen.

Do: reduce plus in 0.25 steps up to the best visual acuity (20/20).

Trap: overshooting into minus. Stop at the most plus / least minus that still gives 20/20. Extra minus looks sharp for a second, then the patient is accommodating to clear your over-minus.

3Ideal fog before the dial

Picture: nudge the image just off the retina again so the astigmatic dial's spokes separate cleanly.

Do: add +0.25 to +0.75 to blur about 2 lines above the best VA, then show the astigmatic (clock) dial.

Trap: going to the dial with no fog. Without the deliberate small blur the spokes look equal and you miss the cylinder.

4Cylinder from the astigmatic dial

Picture: one set of spokes looks darker and sharper because the eye focuses that meridian better. That dark line points along the power, so the correcting minus-cyl axis lies across it, 90° away.

Tentative axis: 90° from the blackest line (or clock rule of 30). Tentative power: add minus cylinder until all spokes are equally dark; for every −0.50 cyl add +0.25 sphere to keep the circle of least confusion on the retina. Target 2 lines above best VA.

Trap: confusing power meridian with axis. The dark line is the power meridian. If asked "is the dark line the axis?" the answer is no.

5Refine cylinder with the JCC

Picture: the cross-cylinder is a tiny flip-lens with a red dot (minus axis) and a white/green dot (plus axis). You flip it and ask "one or two, which is clearer?" and chase the clearer side.

Axis first: put the JCC handle on the tentative axis, flip, ask preference, rotate 5° toward the red of the preferred side (max 15°). Power next: put the red dot on the refined axis, flip, ask; red dot on axis = add cyl, white/green on axis = subtract cyl (keep +0.25 sph per −0.50 cyl).

Trap: if the axis never equalises even past 15°, your fog was wrong, go back. And red dot = minus, always.

6Refine sphere with the duochrome

Picture: the eye focuses green slightly in front of the retina and red slightly behind. Whichever side the letters look bolder tells you which way the focus has drifted.

Do: ask if red and green letters are equally bold. Bolder on red → add minus. Bolder on green → add plus. Endpoint = equal. Then unfog to best VA and repeat the whole loop on OS.

Trap: reversing the direction. Hook it as RAM-GAP: Red Add Minus, Green Add Plus. (Over-corrected myope sees green; under-corrected myope sees red.)

02 Binocular balance (step 7)

Balance equalises accommodation between the two eyes. It is the last step and only when both eyes reach about 20/20.

When you do NOT balance

Skip balancing if the two eyes cannot be compared fairly: amblyopia, suppression, one eye only, ocular pathology, or a presbyope. Examiners love this one.

03 OEP / phorometry: draw a number, name the shape

Universal setup for any drawn VT number: far 6 m / near 40 cm, smallest-readable target, lenses in place = Test #7 (subjective findings), 20 ft-candle, PD to match. Then it is one of four shapes.

The one thing that separates lateral from vertical

Same two prisms, roles swap. Lateral phoria: the 12-15Δ BI on OD measures, endpoint is vertical alignment. Vertical phoria: the 6Δ BU on OS measures, endpoint is horizontal alignment. Recording flips too: lateral is BI=exo/BO=eso; vertical is BU=right hyper.

Demand pairs with reserve (how to reason it, not memorise it)

A phoria is a demand on the fusion system; the vergence reserves are what answers it. Far: an exo demand (#8) is met by convergence reserve (#9 blur / #10 break); an eso demand by divergence (#11). Near: exo (#13B) → #16A blur / #16B break; eso → #17A / #17B. If you forget a number, reason from which direction the eyes must pull.

04 Board traps they slip in

The recurring misses

1. The blackest astigmatic line is the power meridian, not the axis. 2. Duochrome: bolder on red → minus (RAM-GAP). 3. JCC red dot = minus axis. 4. You fog with plus because plus relaxes accommodation. 5. Do not balance in amblyopia / suppression / one eye / pathology / presbyopia. 6. Lateral phoria endpoint = vertical alignment; vertical phoria endpoint = horizontal. 7. Endpoint of subjective = maximum plus / least minus to 20/20. 8. PRA = minus to blur (−2.50); NRA = plus to blur (+2.00). 9. Circle of least confusion is also called the circle of least diffusion. 10. Best night-driving tint = amber/yellow (keeps yellow on the retina, the duochrome logic again).

Do not cross-wire these

LARS (Left Add, Right Subtract) is for toric contact lens rotation, not the JCC or the dial. Rule of 30 is the clock-dial axis shortcut. Keep them in separate drawers.

05 The five they ask (rehearse out loud)

If you drew subjective refraction
  1. Why do you fog with plus? To relax accommodation so you don't over-minus.
  2. The blackest line on the dial is which meridian? The power meridian; the minus-cyl axis is 90° away.
  3. Duochrome: letters clearer on red, what do you do? Add minus (RAM). Principle is transverse chromatic aberration.
  4. What does the red dot on the JCC represent? The minus cylinder axis.
  5. When do you NOT do binocular balance? Amblyopia, suppression, one eye, pathology, presbyopia.
If you drew an OEP / phorometry number
  1. Which prism measures a lateral phoria, and the endpoint? OD 12-15Δ BI measures, OS 6Δ BU dissociates; endpoint vertical alignment.
  2. BO vs BI in vergence testing? BO tests convergence (PRC, #10/#16); BI tests divergence (abduction, NRC, #11/#17).
  3. Normal convergence and abduction at far? #10 = 19/10 (BO); #11 = 9/5 (BI).
  4. Expected PRA and NRA? PRA −2.50 (minus to blur); NRA +2.00 (plus to blur).
  5. How do you get the AC/A by gradient? Change the lens by ±1.00 D and read the phoria shift; 4Δ per 1 D, normal 4:1 min, 5:1 avg.

06 Rebuild it on paper

Reading this is not owning it. Do these blind, pen on paper. That is the retrieval that sticks.

1 · The spine from zero

Write the 7 subjective steps in order, then next to each write the one-line "why" and its trap. Three clean rounds until no peeking.

2 · Draw the three pictures

Sketch: fog in front of the retina; the clock dial with the black line and the axis 90° away; the duochrome green-front / red-behind. Label the move each picture drives.

3 · The 4-archetype grid

Blank columns: archetype, prisms, endpoint, recording. Fill it for lateral phoria, vertical phoria, vergence, accommodation. Then fill the normal-values table from memory.

4 · Direction reflexes

Rapid-fire yourself: red bolder = ? green bolder = ? red dot = ? blackest line = ? PRA = ? NRA = ? lateral endpoint = ? Say each in under 2 seconds.

5 · Teach the loop

Explain to an imaginary examiner why you fog with plus and how the duochrome tells you which way you drifted. If you can teach it, you own it.
