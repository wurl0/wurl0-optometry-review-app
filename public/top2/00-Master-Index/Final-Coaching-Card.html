01 Cornea: the numbers

Transparent, avascular, non-keratinized, and built from orderly collagen. That order is Maurice's lattice theory (1960): fibers evenly spaced so scattered light cancels out. The sclera has an irregular, non-lattice arrangement, which is why it is opaque.

Layers (5 in books, 6 with Dua)

LayerHigh-yield facts
Epithelium~50 µm, regenerates. 5 cell layers of 3 types: surface/squamous 2, wing/umbrella (polyhedral) 2–3, basal/columnar 1 (makes new cells). Basal-to-surface turnover 7 days. Surface cells carry microvilli and microplicae that grip the tear mucin and stabilize the tear film.
Bowman's8–14 µm, anterior limiting lamina. Strong resistance to infection and injury. Scars graded nebula → macula → leucoma.
Stroma (substantia propria)Forward continuation of the sclera. 90% of thickness, ~0.50 mm, ~200 collagen lamellae with keratocytes. Many nonmedullated nerve fibers, so the cornea is the most sensitive structure in the body.
Dua's layerFine pre-Descemet layer, described 2013.
Descemet'sStructureless posterior limiting membrane, secreted by endothelium, resists infection, regenerates. Hassall-Henle warts = peripheral age-related thickenings.
EndotheliumSingle layer of hexagonal cells. Active pump keeps the cornea deturgesced and clear. Does not regenerate. Polymegathism = cells of unequal size from age, disease, trauma, prolonged CL wear.
Framework traps

Feldman and Sampson (1970) count the mucoid layer of the tear film as part of the cornea. To see the endothelium at the slit lamp use specular reflection: angle of illumination = angle of the microscope (Li = Lr).

Before you dispense a prosthetic lens

Slit lamp optic section, oscillating across the opacity. Beam stays straight and clear = smooth surface, dispense. Beam goes crooked and blurred = irregular surface, the lens will pop out; do not dispense.

Non-ionic = neutral, resists protein. Ionic = negative charge, pulls protein.

Right side out?

Bowl test: smooth bowl = correct; flared edges = inverted. Taco test: edges meet = correct; edges repel = inverted. Insertion is direct (looks straight) or indirect (looks up, down, or aside). Removal: indirect pinch after sliding the lens off the cornea, which avoids touching the central cornea.

Judging the fit

A good soft lens covers the whole cornea, centers, and moves after a blink.

Why the blink flips it

A loose lens slides after a blink, so vision blurs right after. A tight lens is briefly pushed into a better shape by the lid, so vision clears only right after the blink. In the practical, say "optimum, loose, or restricted" unless you actually measured the mm.

Tinted soft lenses

Handling (visibility) tint: light tint to find the lens and tell eyes apart (e.g., OD −5.00 green, OS −1.50 blue). Cosmetic opaque / prosthetic: solid iris with clear or black pupil. Enhancing tint: deepens or changes iris color.

06 Toric lenses

Different power in each meridian, so the lens must hold its axis. Normal nasal kick on blinking = 3–5°. More than 5° is not acceptable; compensate the axis using the scribe marks.

LARS

CATS

Which toric design

Residual astigmatism (Ra) = total (refractive) astigmatism − corneal astigmatism (AC). Corneal astig comes from K readings; total from subjective refraction.

Worked examples from the lecture

BST: K 44.00 / 46.00 → AC −2.00 × 90; Rx pl −0.50 × 90. Ra = 0.50 − 2.00 = 1.50 D; cornea carries more astig, Ra under 3 → BST.
Bitoric: AC −4.00 × 180; Rx pl −0.50 × 180. Ra = 3.50 D, over 3 → bitoric.

07 RGP fitting

Corneal GP 8–11 mm, ~2.8% fluid absorption, VLT 98–99%, masks irregular astigmatism. No fit factor is added to a computed RGP base curve; use the computed value. Let the lens settle ~20 minutes, then check fluorescein under cobalt blue.

One line

More minus = steep. Less minus = flat. Expected = optimum. The steep lens made a plus tear lens, so you need extra minus to cancel it.

Tear lens math

Every 0.1 mm BC vs K difference = 0.50 D. Steeper than K = plus (+ sign); flatter = minus (− sign). Cornea 7.8, BC 7.9 → 0.1 flatter → −0.50 D.

Astigmatic patterns

Visible from ~0.75 D corneal astig; dye pools along the steep meridian. WTR = H / dumbbell (pools top-bottom). ATR = "eye" or I pattern (pools nasal-temporal).

Thickness and edges

At about −3.00 D, center thickness is 0.10 mm PMMA and 0.16 mm CAB. A lenticular flange changes the front edge thickness for better lid pickup. High minus has a thick edge → thin it with a hyperflange / CN bevel. High plus (or low minus) has a thin edge → thicken it with a myoflange.

Axial length ↔ power: 1 mm = 3.00 D

Axial myopia: AL = 24 + (Rx ÷ 3). Axial hyperopia: AL = 24 − (Rx ÷ 3). Backwards: (AL − 24) × 3, myopic if AL > 24, hyperopic if AL < 24. Example: −6.00 D → 26 mm; 22 mm → about +6.00 D.

11 Uvea and retina

Uvea = choroid, iris, ciliary body. The choroid is dark, very vascular, covers ~5/6 of the inner eye, and regulates ocular temperature.

Choroid, outside inContent
1. suprachoroid (lamina fusca)elastic flattened laminae next to sclera
2. stromaHaller = large vessels (outer) · Sattler = medium vessels
3. choriocapillarissmall capillaries, nourish the outer retina
4. Bruch's membranebasement membrane between RPE and choriocapillaris

Iris: pupillary zone (sphincter) · ciliary zone (dilator) · collarette divides them and is the thickest part · crypts = stromal openings. Layers, roughly the reverse of the cornea: anterior border layer, anterior limiting membrane, stroma (sphincter, nerves, pigment = iris color), posterior limiting membrane, pigmented epithelium. Pupil 3.5–5 mm, slightly nasal and inferior; light = constriction; rhythmic pulsing = hippus.

Ciliary body: ring ~6 mm wide, scleral spur to ora serrata. Muscle fibers: longitudinal (toward pars plana), radial (middle), circular (near the processes). Main job: accommodation.

Retina: inner nervous coat, 0.1–0.5 mm. Firmly attached at ora serrata, optic disc, fovea (not the choroid). Vitreous is its support cushion.

Visual colors

Violet = iodopsin · purple = rhodopsin · yellow = retinene (rhodopsin bleaches to retinene + vitamin A).

Light adaptation (dark → light, leaving the cinema) is faster. Dark adaptation (entering the cinema) is slower. Retinal changes in light: migratory (pigment moves toward the ELM, cone myoids shorten), electrical (rods and cones go negative), chemical (pH 7.3 in dark → 7.0 in light).

13 Accommodation

Physical accommodation = actual lens change, in diopters. Physiological = ciliary muscle effort, in myodiopters (not measured clinically).

TheoryIdea
Helmholtz, decreased tensionciliary contracts → zonules relax → lens rounds up
Tscherning, increased tensionciliary action increases zonular tension; lens goes conoidal / hyperboloid

During accommodation the lens moves forward, thickens 3.6 → 4.0 mm, anterior radius steepens 10 → 5.3 mm, power rises 19–20 → 33 D, equatorial diameter decreases, and the anterior chamber gets shallower.

Hofstetter vs Donders

Stem says minimum, average, or maximum → Hofstetter: min 15 − 0.25(age), avg 18.5 − 0.30(age), max 25 − 0.40(age). Only age and amplitude, none of those words → Donders table.

Torsion and labels

The 12 o’clock point of the cornea (the lecture calls it the anterior pole) turns out = extorsion, in = intorsion. Reverse it for the posterior pole. Identify axes by the movement, not the X-Y-Z letter; books swap letters.

Angle alpha: optic vs visual axis at the nodal point. Angle gamma: optic vs fixation axis at the center of rotation. Angle kappa: visual vs pupillary axis. Straight eyes 0; positive kappa looks exotropic, negative kappa looks esotropic.

19 Spaced-repetition deck

  1. Average corneal power and central thickness? 43.27 D; 540 µm (0.54 mm).
  2. Anterior vs posterior corneal radius? 7.8 mm (flatter) vs 6.5 mm (steeper).
  3. Epithelial turnover time? 7 days, basal to surface.
  4. Which corneal layer never regenerates? Endothelium (hexagonal pump cells).
  5. Polymegathism? Unequal endothelial cell size: age, disease, trauma, long CL wear.
  6. Corneal O₂ with CL, eyes closed? 0%, hypoxia.
  7. Keratometer measures what zone? Central 3 mm, anterior surface.
  8. Elevation map cannot come from? A Placido-only topographer.
  9. Keratoconus cutoffs? K ≥ 47.00 D, pachymetry under 540 µm.
  10. Munson vs Rizzuti? Lower lid V on downgaze vs nasal limbal reflex from temporal light.
  11. Piggyback vs hybrid? Two lenses (soft under RGP) vs one lens (RGP center, soft skirt).
  12. PMMA, CAB, HEMA indices? 1.49, 1.47, 1.42.
  13. Theoretical OAD, soft vs RGP? HVID + 2.5 mm vs HVID − 2.5 mm.
  14. Adaptation schedule, new conventional wearer? 2, 4, 6, 8 hours on days 1–4, then max wear.
  15. Solid iris with black pupil is for? Central opacity in a blind eye.
  16. FDA soft Group IV? High water, ionic: most protein and jelly bumps.
