Card02Bioethics

Four principles, and the patient's rights

Ethics questions are answered from the patient's side. Four principles anchor every case, and they resolve into concrete rights and duties: consent, confidentiality, referral, and honest judgment. Learn them and the scenario items pick themselves.

02 Patient rights, in practice

RightWhat it means
Informed consentTold the risks and benefits before agreeing to treatment
Informed refusalMay decline a procedure after being informed (e.g. dilation)
Confidentiality / privacyNo disclosure without patient authorization or a legal requirement, even to an employer or family
Access to records / prescriptionThe record and the spectacle Rx belong to the patient; release for a second opinion with proper authorization
Confidentiality trap

An employer paying for the exam, or simply requesting records, does not override confidentiality. Release only with the patient's authorization or when the law requires it.

03 The optometrist's duties

Judgment and referral

Your primary responsibility is the visual welfare of the public, above revenue. Keep independent professional judgment: refuse a clinically inappropriate demand (an unnecessary drug, an Rx without an exam). And refer when it exceeds your scope, a suspected retinal detachment goes promptly to the specialist.

Ending a relationship, safely

You may end care with an abusive patient, but never abandon. Terminate appropriately, with notice and continuity of care (records, a referral). Abandonment breaches non-maleficence.

04 Professional conduct and misconduct

Keep your own house in order

Maintain competence through continuing development, comply with the law, and hold a valid certificate and PIC. No self-laudatory advertising or solicitation. If you discover a colleague altering records for fraud, report it through the proper professional or legal channels, not privately ignored, and not on social media.

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. The four bioethical principles? Autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice.
  2. Autonomy in practice? The patient decides: informed consent and informed refusal.
  3. Non-maleficence? First, do no harm (and do not abandon a patient).
  4. The optometrist's primary responsibility? To protect the visual welfare of the public.
  5. Duty to refer? Refer promptly when a condition exceeds your scope (e.g., suspected retinal detachment).
  6. Confidentiality and an employer's request? Maintain it unless the patient authorizes disclosure or the law requires it.
  7. Informed consent vs informed refusal? Consent = agreeing after hearing risks/benefits; refusal = declining after being informed.
  8. Patient demands an Rx without an exam? Refuse and explain the need for a complete examination (independent judgment).
  9. Abusive patient? Terminate the relationship appropriately while ensuring continuity of care (never abandon).
  10. Records for a second opinion? Release with the patient's proper authorization.
  11. Colleague altering records for fraud? Report through the proper professional/legal channels, professionally.
  12. A patient information system protecting health data best demonstrates? Patient confidentiality and privacy.
