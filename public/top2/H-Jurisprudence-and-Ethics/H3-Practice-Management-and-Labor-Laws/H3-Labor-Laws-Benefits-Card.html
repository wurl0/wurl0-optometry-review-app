Card04Labor laws

The benefits the law guarantees your staff

As an employer you owe three mandatory contributions, statutory leaves, and separation pay when you end a job for an authorized cause. These are floors, not favors, you cannot go below them. Learn the three funds, the leaves, and the DOLE remedy.

01 The three mandatory contributions

Hooks

SSS = the safety net (retirement/disability). PhilHealth = health insurance, made universal by RA 11223 (Universal Health Care Act). Pag-IBIG = the housing fund. The PhilHealth premium is rate x monthly salary, not age or number of dependents.

02 Ending a job: separation pay

Authorized causes owe separation pay

When you end a job for an authorized cause, retrenchment, redundancy, closure, or installing labor-saving devices, the employee is owed separation pay. So a worker retrenched after years of service gets separation pay. (Termination for a just cause, serious misconduct, does not.)

03 Leaves, and the benefits floor

BenefitEntitlement
Expanded maternity leave (RA 11210)105 days of paid leave
Service incentive leave5 paid days a year (after 1 year)
13th month pay, holiday payStatutory monetary benefits
You cannot go below the law

These are minimums. An employer who grants only 60 days of maternity leave when the law says 105 is in violation, "company policy" does not override a statutory right.

04 When a benefit is denied: DOLE

The remedy

If an employer shortchanges a statutory benefit, the employee should file a complaint or seek assistance from the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE). That is the correct channel, not silent acceptance and not simply resigning.

Employment vs independent practice

A recognized disadvantage of corporate/employed optometric practice is limits on the scope of practice. On the tax side, an optometrist employed only by the government is exempt from the professional tax.

07 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. The three mandatory contributions? SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG (employer and employee both pay).
  2. What does SSS cover? Retirement, sickness, disability, and death benefits.
  3. PhilHealth and which law? Health insurance (hospital and outpatient), made universal by RA 11223 (Universal Health Care).
  4. PhilHealth contribution basis? The applicable rate times the employee's monthly salary base.
  5. Pag-IBIG? The housing/savings fund (home loans).
  6. Which terminations owe separation pay? Authorized causes (retrenchment, redundancy, closure), not just causes.
  7. Retrenchment after years of service? Separation pay is owed.
  8. Expanded maternity leave length and law? 105 days paid, under RA 11210.
  9. Employer grants only 60 days of maternity leave? A violation; a statutory benefit cannot be reduced by company policy.
  10. Employee shortchanged on a benefit? File a complaint or seek assistance from DOLE.
  11. Disadvantage of corporate/employed practice? Limitations on scope of practice.
  12. Who is exempt from the professional tax? An optometrist employed exclusively by the government.
