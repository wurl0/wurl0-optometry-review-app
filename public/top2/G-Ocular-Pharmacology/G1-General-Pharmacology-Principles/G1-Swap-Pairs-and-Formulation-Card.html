These are the pairs where both terms are known but assigned to the wrong one. Drill the contrast, not the fact.

Absorption — how it crosses the membrane

MechanismBoard clue
Simple diffusionlipid soluble, most drugs, most common
Active transportelectrolyte, sodium, ion, against gradient (energy)
Facilitated diffusioncarrier protein, no energy, glucose
Filtrationsmall, water channels, pores

Interaction terms — the arithmetic

TermRule
Additive1 + 1 = 2 (both active, equals the sum)
Synergism1 + 1 > 2 (both active, beats the sum): tropicamide + phenylephrine
Potentiation1 + 0 > 1 (one inactive booster): lidocaine + hyaluronidase
Antagonismreverses / opposes: atropine vs organophosphate

Prescription parts — who it is for

PartFor
Superscriptionthe heading (Rx symbol, patient, date)
Inscriptionthe body: drug name, strength, quantity
Subscriptionto the pharmacist (how to dispense)
Signatura (Sig)to the patient (how to use)

Indication classes — why it is given

ClassMeaning + example
Protectivereplaces/maintains a body need: insulin for life
Curativeeliminates the cause: acyclovir for varicella
Prophylacticprevents disease not yet present: antibiotic after surgery
Palliativerelieves symptom only: naproxen for pain
Diagnosticto diagnose: cocaine test in Horner's
PK vs PD: PK = what the body does to the drug (ADME). PD = what the drug does to the body (effect, potency, therapeutic index).
Branches: pharmacognosy = crude/natural source (guava leaves) · pharmacotherapy = using drugs to treat disease.

Reference (answer key)

An eye drop is the drug plus helpers that keep it dissolved, stable, sterile, and comfortable.

Solution components — the job

ComponentJob / example
Active ingredientthe drug that works (dorzolamide)
Vehicledissolves and carries the drug, controls viscosity, aids transport
Buffering agentholds the pH steady (boric acid, borate, phosphate)
Preservativestops microbial growth (benzalkonium chloride, BAK)
Antioxidant / chelatorprevents oxidation, grabs calcium/metals (EDTA)
Excipientinert filler, gives form/consistency, limited use in eye drops
Surfactantlowers surface tension, wetting

pH values

ValueApplies to
pH ~6stability pH of most ophthalmic solutions and DPAs
pH 7.4physiologic tear pH (comfort, not stability)

Micelle vs reverse micelle

StructureCore faces in / traps
Micelle (in water)tails inward, traps an oil droplet
Reverse micelle (in oil)heads inward, traps a water droplet
Anchor: likes hide from what they dislike. The core always holds the opposite of the surrounding fluid.
Traps you fell into: BAK is preservative + wetting + disinfectant, NOT a lubricant. Vehicle dissolves/carries the drug; excipient just gives form (barely used in drops).
