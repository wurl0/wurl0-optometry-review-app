Card07G1 principles

G1 general principles: the words that trade places

On the MCU preboard, most of the G1 misses were not blank spots. They were twins: two terms you knew, assigned to the wrong one. Absorption routes, interaction terms, prescription parts, indication classes. This card lines each pair up so the difference, not the word, is what you drill. It closes with the one true gap: what is actually inside an eye drop.

01 Why these miss (and how to stop it)

A definition item feels easy, so you read "pharmacognosy," think "yes, I know that word," and mark it right in your head. But knowing a word is not the same as pointing it at the correct meaning. When two terms live in the same family, familiarity fails you: both feel right, and you pick the wrong twin.

The fix in one line

Never study a swap-term alone. Study it against its twin, and memorize the one phrase that separates them. On this card that phrase is the "board clue" in every table.

02 How a drug crosses the membrane

The cell membrane is a fatty sheet. Fat-friendly drugs slip through it on their own. Salts cannot, so a pump spends energy to haul them across. That single picture decides most absorption questions.

MechanismEnergyDirectionBoard clue
Simple diffusionNoDown gradientlipid soluble, most drugs, "most common"
Active transportYesAgainst gradientelectrolyte, sodium, ion
Facilitated diffusionNoDown gradientcarrier protein, glucose
FiltrationNoBulk flowsmall, water-soluble, through pores
The exact flip you made

You put active transport for the lipid-soluble drug and simple diffusion for sodium. Reverse it: lipid = simple diffusion, ion = active transport.

03 Interaction terms, as arithmetic

Two drugs together. Treat their combined effect like a sum, and the four terms sort themselves out. The only hard split is synergism versus potentiation, and it turns on one question: is the second drug active on its own?

TermArithmeticBoth drugs active?Example
Additive1 + 1 = 2Yeseffect equals the sum
Synergism1 + 1 > 2Yes, bothtropicamide + phenylephrine; apraclonidine + pilocarpine
Potentiation1 + 0 > 1No, one is a boosterlidocaine + hyaluronidase
Antagonism1 + (−1)One opposesatropine vs organophosphate
Synergism vs potentiation

If both drugs work toward the same effect and the total beats the sum, it is synergism. If one drug does little or nothing alone and only amplifies the other, it is potentiation. Hyaluronidase is not an anesthetic; it just spreads the lidocaine, so that pair is potentiation.

04 Parts of the prescription order

One prescription talks to four readers. Read it top to bottom and each part names its audience.

PartForContains
SuperscriptionheadingRx symbol, patient, date
Inscriptionthe bodydrug name, strength, quantity
Subscriptionthe pharmacisthow to dispense
Signatura (Sig)the patienthow and when to use
The exact miss

Body = inscription (you put transcription, which is not a classic part). Instruction to the patient = signatura (you put superscription, which is the heading).

05 Indication classes: why is it given?

This is where "palliative" was over-firing. Palliative is correct only when the drug does nothing but ease a symptom. Ask that first.

ClassWhat the drug doesExample
Protectivereplaces / maintains a body needinsulin for life in DM
Curativeeliminates the causeacyclovir for varicella
Prophylacticprevents a disease not yet presentantibiotic after phaco
Palliativerelieves a symptom onlynaproxen for pain
Diagnostichelps make a diagnosiscocaine in Horner's
Empiricalgiven before the cause is confirmedantibiotic before culture
The exact miss

Insulin lifelong = protective (replacement). Antibiotic after surgery = prophylactic (prevents infection). You chose palliative for both. If it is not purely symptom relief, it is not palliative.

06 PK vs PD, and the branch names

One rule kills a whole family of items: PK is what the body does to the drug; PD is what the drug does to the body.

TermMeaningBoard clue
Pharmacokineticsbody acts on drugabsorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion (ADME)
Pharmacodynamicsdrug acts on bodyeffect, potency, efficacy, therapeutic index
Pharmacognosydrugs from natural sourcescrude, plant, "guava leaves"
Pharmacotherapyusing drugs to treat disease"application to treat"
Posologystudy of dosage"dose"
The exact miss

Therapeutic index is pharmacodynamics (you picked biotransformation, which is PK). Pharmacologic effect is not part of pharmacokinetics.

07 What is actually in an eye drop

This was the one true content gap, not a swap. An eye drop is the drug plus a set of helpers that keep it dissolved, stable, sterile, and comfortable. Learn the job of each helper and the questions answer themselves.

ComponentJobExample
Active ingredientdoes the workdorzolamide
Vehicledissolves + carries the drug, controls viscositypurified water base
Buffering agentholds pH steadyboric acid, borate
Preservativestops microbial growthbenzalkonium chloride (BAK)
Antioxidant / chelatorprevents oxidation, grabs calciumEDTA
Excipientinert filler, gives form; limited use in dropsinert base
Surfactantlowers surface tension, wettingpolysorbate
Two traps you fell into

BAK is not a lubricant. It is a preservative, wetting agent, and disinfectant. And do not swap vehicle (dissolves and carries the drug) with excipient (just gives form, barely used in eye drops).

pH numbers

Formulate for stability at pH ~6 (this is the answer the board wants, and what you missed twice by choosing 7). Do not confuse it with tear pH 7.4, which is comfort, not stability.

08 Micelle vs reverse micelle

A surfactant molecule has a water-loving head and an oil-loving tail. When many gather, they hide from whatever they dislike, and the core always holds the opposite of the surrounding fluid.

Hook

Likes hide from what they dislike. In water, the oil-loving tails huddle inside and trap oil. In oil, the water-loving heads huddle inside and trap water.

11 Spaced-repetition deck

Drop these into your FoqusLab review queue on the 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Lipid-soluble drug absorption vs sodium absorption? Simple diffusion (lipid); active transport (sodium).
  2. Synergism vs potentiation? Both active, effect beats the sum = synergism; one inactive booster amplifies the other = potentiation.
  3. Body of the prescription vs instruction to the patient? Inscription (body); signatura (patient).
  4. Lifelong insulin in DM is which indication class? Protective (replacement), not palliative.
  5. Antibiotic after phaco is which class? Prophylactic (prevents infection).
  6. Therapeutic index is PK or PD? Pharmacodynamics.
  7. One-line PK vs PD? PK = body acts on drug (ADME); PD = drug acts on body (effect, potency).
  8. Which is NOT a use of BAK? Lubricant (it is a preservative, wetting agent, disinfectant).
  9. Vehicle vs excipient? Vehicle dissolves and carries the drug; excipient just gives form, barely used in drops.
  10. Stability pH of an eye drop? About 6 (tear pH 7.4 is comfort, not stability).
  11. Micelle in water vs reverse micelle? Tails inward trapping oil; heads inward trapping water.
