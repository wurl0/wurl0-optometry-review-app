Card03Therapeutic set

Anti-inflammatories: cut the cascade, then allergy and dry eye

Steroids and NSAIDs both quiet inflammation, but they cut the same pathway at different points, and that one difference explains every side effect the board tests. Learn the cascade, then allergy and dry eye fall in behind it as the surface problems you calm the same way.

01 One cascade, two cuts

Inflammation runs down the arachidonic acid cascade. Steroids cut it high, NSAIDs cut it low, and where the cut lands is why one is powerful and dangerous and the other is safe but limited.

Hook

High cut, high power, high risk. Steroids block the top of the cascade, so they shut down everything and work in almost any inflammation, but that reach is also why they raise pressure, cloud the lens, and let infection run. NSAIDs cut lower and only touch prostaglandins, so they are safer but weaker.

02 Corticosteroids

Potency tracks penetration, and so does the pressure risk. The two low-IOP steroids are the ones that either hydrolyze fast or barely get in.

The steroid triad, plus one

Topical steroids raise IOP in responders, grow a posterior subcapsular cataract, and let infection run (HSV, fungus, Acanthamoeba, from Card 2). Add delayed wound healing. Reach for a soft steroid (loteprednol) or FML when you need weeks of use, and watch the pressure either way.

Where steroids are the answer

Anterior uveitis and iritis (the mainstay), post-op inflammation, severe allergy (VKC/AKC, short pulses), scleritis, and immune stromal keratitis. The rule from Card 2 still holds, never on an undiagnosed red eye.

03 NSAIDs

Same job, lower cut. Weaker than steroids, but they skip the pressure, the cataract, and the infection risk entirely.

AgentWhere it shines

The NSAID risk

Topical NSAIDs can cause a sterile corneal melt (keratolysis), especially in a dry or already-compromised cornea. Great for a normal post-op eye, risky on a sick surface. That single danger is the mirror image of the steroid list: no pressure, no cataract, no infection, but watch the melt.

Steroid or NSAID?

Need power and can watch the pressure, steroid. Want to avoid the IOP and cataract risk, or you are treating pain and cystoid macular edema after surgery, NSAID. Many post-op regimens run both.

04 Allergy: stop the mast cell

Ocular allergy is one symptom above all, itch. It is a type I reaction: the mast cell degranulates, histamine pours out, the eye itches, waters, and reddens on both sides. The drugs either block the histamine, stop the degranulation, or both.

ClassMechanismOnsetExamples
AntihistamineBlock H1Fastemedastine
Mast cell stabilizerPrevent degranulationSlow, prophylacticcromolyn, lodoxamide, nedocromil
Dual-actionBoth, first-lineFast + preventiveolopatadine, ketotifen, bepotastine, epinastine, alcaftadine
DecongestantAlpha vasoconstrictorFast (redness only)naphazoline, tetrahydrozoline
Two allergy traps

Dual-action agents are first-line for allergic conjunctivitis, fast relief now and prevention with continued use. Decongestants rebound: chronic naphazoline causes conjunctivitis medicamentosa, redder than when you started. And itch is the tell, if it does not itch, rethink allergy.

Know the severe forms

VKC (vernal): young males, giant cobblestone tarsal papillae, shield ulcer, Horner-Trantas dots. AKC (atopic): older, eczema, scarring risk. GPC: contact lens or prosthesis mechanical trigger. Severe VKC/AKC earn short steroid pulses (loteprednol preferred).

05 Dry eye, treated as inflammation

Two flavors: aqueous-deficient (the lacrimal gland underproduces, think Sjogren) and evaporative (meibomian gland dysfunction, the common one). Modern treatment climbs a ladder and treats the surface inflammation, not just the dryness.

Step 1
Lubricants / artificial tears
First-line; preservative-free for frequent dosing
Step 2
Cyclosporine 0.05% · Lifitegrast 5%
Anti-inflammatory, raise tear production / block T-cells; both take weeks to work
Step 2b
Short topical steroid pulse
Calms a flare while the slow agents ramp up
MGD arm
Warm compress, lid hygiene, oral doxycycline/azithromycin, omega-3
For the evaporative (meibomian) type, the usual culprit
Conserve
Punctal plugs
Keep the tears you have; best for aqueous-deficient eyes
Why cyclosporine feels like it is failing

Cyclosporine and lifitegrast work by quieting T-cell-driven surface inflammation, which takes weeks, so patients quit early thinking they do nothing. Set the expectation, and bridge a bad flare with a short steroid. This is the same anti-inflammatory logic as the rest of the card, aimed at the ocular surface.

07 Rebuild it on paper

1 · Draw the cascade

Phospholipid to arachidonic acid to prostaglandins. Mark where steroids cut and where NSAIDs cut, and write what each one spares.

2 · Steroid side effects, safer agents

List the steroid triad plus wound healing, then name the two low-IOP steroids and the reason each is gentler on pressure.

3 · Rank the allergy classes

Order antihistamine, mast cell stabilizer, dual-action, and decongestant by onset. Circle the first-line and write why.

4 · Dry eye ladder blind

Rebuild the five rungs from memory, and split aqueous-deficient from evaporative with one treatment unique to each.

5 · Teach steroid vs NSAID

Explain when you pick each, and name the one risk that belongs only to each (steroid: IOP/cataract/infection; NSAID: corneal melt).

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Steroid mechanism vs NSAID mechanism? Steroids block phospholipase A2 (whole cascade, prostaglandins and leukotrienes); NSAIDs block COX (prostaglandins only).
  2. Steroid side-effect triad, plus one? Raised IOP (responders), posterior subcapsular cataract, worsened infection; plus delayed wound healing.
  3. Two low-IOP steroids and why? Loteprednol (ester, hydrolyzes fast) and fluorometholone (low penetration).
  4. Prednisolone acetate formulation caution? It is a suspension, shake before use or you underdose.
  5. The one NSAID risk? Sterile corneal melt (keratolysis), especially in a dry or compromised cornea.
  6. NSAID advantages over steroids? No IOP rise, no cataract, no infection risk.
  7. First-line drug class for allergic conjunctivitis? Dual-action antihistamine plus mast cell stabilizer (olopatadine, ketotifen).
  8. Hallmark symptom of ocular allergy? Itching; watery or stringy mucus, bilateral.
  9. Problem with chronic decongestant use? Rebound redness (conjunctivitis medicamentosa).
  10. First-line dry eye, and the two anti-inflammatory drops with their onset? Lubricants first; cyclosporine 0.05% and lifitegrast 5%, both take weeks.
  11. Most common dry eye type and its non-drop treatments? Evaporative (MGD): warm compress, lid hygiene, oral doxycycline/azithromycin, omega-3.
  12. VKC hallmark signs? Young males, giant cobblestone tarsal papillae, shield ulcer, Horner-Trantas dots.
