Card06Systemic effects

Systemic drugs on the eye: learn the target

A pill taken for the heart, the lungs, or the mind can land on one eye structure and leave a signature there. Do not memorize pharmacology, memorize which drug hits which tissue, and the finding names itself. This closes the Ocular Pharma set.

03 The four the board tests hardest

Hydroxychloroquine, and why screening exists

Bull's-eye maculopathy is late and irreversible, so the whole point is to catch damage before it. Keep the dose under 5 mg/kg/day, examine at baseline, then screen every year after 5 years (sooner with renal disease, tamoxifen use, or high dose). The two screening tests are a 10-2 automated visual field and SD-OCT (parafoveal thinning). Stop the drug on the earliest change, not when the bull's-eye appears.

Ethambutol optic neuropathy

Dose and duration dependent. The earliest sign is red-green color loss, then a central or cecocentral scotoma and falling acuity, usually bilateral. Monitor color vision and acuity; it is reversible if the drug is stopped early. Isoniazid can do the same, which is why B6 goes with it.

Topiramate: not your usual angle-closure

Sudden bilateral angle-closure with an acute myopic shift, from a ciliochoroidal effusion pushing the lens and iris forward. That bilateral, younger, myopic picture separates it from primary angle-closure (unilateral, older, hyperopic, pupillary block). Stop the drug, use cycloplegia and aqueous suppressants, and know that a laser iridotomy does not help here, because the mechanism is not pupillary block.

Steroids, the recurring villain

Systemic or topical, steroids give the same trio you met in Cards 3 and 4: posterior subcapsular cataract, raised IOP, and central serous chorioretinopathy. If a stem stacks cataract plus pressure plus a serous macular detachment, think steroid exposure.

04 The color-vision trio

Three drugs, three colors

Digoxin paints the world yellow (xanthopsia), with halos, a sign of toxicity. Sildenafil tints it blue (cyanopsia), transient. Rifampin turns the tears orange and stains soft lenses. Yellow-heart, blue-pill, orange-tears.

06 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Amiodarone's most common ocular effect? Cornea verticillata (whorl keratopathy); usually asymptomatic and reversible.
  2. Hydroxychloroquine retinal toxicity and screening? Bull's-eye maculopathy (late, irreversible); baseline then annual after 5 years, dose under 5 mg/kg, screen with 10-2 field + SD-OCT.
  3. Ethambutol ocular toxicity and earliest sign? Toxic optic neuropathy; earliest is red-green color loss, then central/cecocentral scotoma; reversible if caught early.
  4. Topiramate ocular emergency? Acute bilateral angle-closure with an acute myopic shift (ciliochoroidal effusion); iridotomy does not help.
  5. How does topiramate angle-closure differ from primary? Bilateral, sudden, younger, with a myopic shift; primary is unilateral, older, hyperopic, pupillary block.
  6. Three eye effects of systemic steroids? Posterior subcapsular cataract, raised IOP, central serous chorioretinopathy.
  7. Color trio: digoxin, sildenafil, rifampin? Digoxin yellow (xanthopsia), sildenafil blue (cyanopsia), rifampin orange tears (stains soft lenses).
  8. Tamsulosin surgical risk? Intraoperative floppy iris syndrome during cataract surgery.
  9. Thioridazine toxicity? Pigmentary retinopathy (high dose).
  10. Tamoxifen retinal finding? Crystalline retinopathy and cystoid macular edema.
  11. Isotretinoin ocular effects? Dry eye, blepharoconjunctivitis, reduced night vision.
  12. Vigabatrin visual effect? Irreversible peripheral visual field constriction.
