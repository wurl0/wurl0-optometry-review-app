Card08Drug tox

Drugs leave fingerprints: signs, colors, and pupils

A whole band of pharma items on the preboard were "which drug does this to the eye." They are pure recognition: each drug stamps a specific mark on the cornea, lens, color vision, or pupil. Learn the fingerprint and the drug names itself. This card also covers the two safety families the board loves: drugs of abuse (by pupil) and drugs in pregnancy.

You put ethambutol as blue-green and digitalis as red-green. Reverse: ethambutol = red-green, digitalis = blue-yellow. Ethanol sits with digitalis on blue-yellow.

The exact miss

Whorl-like cornea = chloroquine (you put digitalis; digitalis hits color vision, not the cornea). And corticosteroids' ocular effects are PSC cataract, glaucoma, dilated pupil. Hyperglycemia is a systemic steroid effect, so it is the odd one out in an "ocular effect except" stem.

"Except" logic

Oral contraceptives cause vascular/thrombotic problems, so in an "ocular side effect except" stem the answer is the non-vascular refractive option (hyperopia). Read "except" items by finding the one that does not fit the mechanism.

04 Band keratopathy: the calcium company

Band keratopathy is calcium laid down in the cornea, so its causes are the things that raise calcium or chronically inflame the eye.

The exact miss

"Not a cause of band keratopathy" = hyperthyroidism (you put excess vitamin D, which DOES cause it via hypercalcemia). Anchor: band keratopathy = calcium, and thyroid is not a calcium problem.

05 Toxidromes: read the pupil

Two drug families, opposite pupils. This is a give-away once you anchor it: opioids constrict, stimulants dilate.

The exact miss

You marked morphine as dilated, even though you correctly called heroin and meperidine constricted. They are all opioids, so all three give miosis. Same class = same pupil. Cocaine and methamphetamine are stimulants = mydriasis.

Also here

Most commonly abused OTC drug = acetaminophen (paracetamol). Most abused non-OTC antibiotic = amoxicillin.

06 Drugs in pregnancy

Antibioticampicillin (penicillins)tetracycline (stains teeth, bone)

Why insulin, why not tetracycline

Insulin is a large protein that does not cross the placenta, so it is the safe glucose drug in pregnancy. Tetracycline chelates calcium in developing teeth and bone, so it is contraindicated. These two show up as "safe in pregnancy" stems every cycle.

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Drop these into your FoqusLab review queue on the 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Ethambutol color defect? Red-green (plus optic neuritis).
  2. Digitalis color defect? Blue-yellow (xanthopsia, yellow vision).
  3. Whorl-like corneal deposits (cornea verticillata)? Chloroquine / hydroxychloroquine (also amiodarone).
  4. Ocular effects of corticosteroids? Posterior subcapsular cataract, secondary open-angle glaucoma, dilated pupil (hyperglycemia is systemic).
  5. Ocular side effect of hydrochlorothiazide? Dry eyes.
  6. Oral contraceptive ocular risk, and the "except"? Retinal vein/artery occlusion, CL intolerance; not hyperopia.
  7. Systemic toxicity of parenteral aminoglycosides? Ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity.
  8. Which does NOT cause band keratopathy? Hyperthyroidism (the rest raise calcium).
  9. Opioids do what to the pupil? Miosis (morphine, heroin, meperidine).
  10. Stimulants do what to the pupil? Mydriasis (cocaine, methamphetamine).
  11. Most commonly abused OTC drug? Acetaminophen.
  12. Safe antibiotic and safe glucose drug in pregnancy? Ampicillin; insulin. Avoid tetracycline and metformin.
