Card07Clinical fixes

Clinical fixes: color, low vision, and the diabetic fundus

The clinical misses split into three tight families: naming color-vision defects, the low-vision definition-and-magnification chain, and the diabetic retinopathy signs. The contact-lens items, which were the bulk, are consolidated on the Theoretical Optics card (Card 7) since contact lenses live there. This card owns the rest.

01 Color-vision defects: name by cone and by severity

Two dimensions name every defect: which cone (L=red=prot, M=green=deuter, S=blue=trit) and how bad (anomaly = weak, anopia = absent).

StemAnswer
red cones with decreased but not absent sensitivityprotanomaly
the most common type of dichromacydeuteranopia
the most accurate color-vision assessment toolanomaloscope
inheritance of complete achromatopsiaautosomal recessive
achromatopsia type affecting only menincomplete (blue-cone) achromatopsia, X-linked
Anchors

anomaly = weak cone; anopia = absent cone. Deutan (green) defects are the commonest overall. The anomaloscope is the gold standard (it forces a Rayleigh red-green match). Complete achromatopsia (rod monochromatism) is autosomal recessive; the men-only one is the X-linked blue-cone type.

02 Low vision: the definition chain and the magnifier math

Term / taskAnswer
result of an ocular abnormalityvisual disorder
WHO visual-field limit for blindness (better eye)less than 20 degrees
vision-related change in a person's skills and abilitiesvisual disability
reading distance through a +20.00 D spectacle magnifier5 cm
20/100 target seen through 5X magnificationequivalent to 20/500
The two calculations

A plus magnifier's working distance is its focal length: 100 / 20 D = 5 cm. Magnification multiplies the effective target size: a 20/100 letter under 5X behaves like 20/500 (5 x 100). Disorder to impairment to disability to handicap is the WHO severity chain.

03 Diabetic retinopathy signs

SignMeaning
Microaneurysmsearliest clinical sign; capillary outpouchings
Dot-and-blot hemorrhagesthe characteristic DR hemorrhage shape
Cotton-wool spotsnerve-fiber-layer microinfarcts (ischemia)
Neovascularizationdefines proliferative diabetic retinopathy
The one that upgrades the stage

New vessels are the line between non-proliferative and proliferative DR. Microaneurysms come first, cotton-wool spots mark ischemia, and neovascularization is the dangerous late sign. This overlaps your E3 diabetic-retinopathy fundus signature.

05 Spaced-repetition deck

  1. Red cones weak, not absent? Protanomaly (anomaly = weak).
  2. Most common dichromacy? Deuteranopia.
  3. Most accurate color-vision test? Anomaloscope.
  4. Complete achromatopsia inheritance? Autosomal recessive.
  5. Achromatopsia affecting only men? X-linked blue-cone (incomplete) type.
  6. WHO field limit for blindness? Less than 20 degrees in the better eye.
  7. Working distance of a +20 D magnifier? 5 cm (100/20).
  8. 20/100 under 5X equals? 20/500.
  9. Earliest clinical sign of DR? Microaneurysms.
  10. What defines proliferative DR? Neovascularization.
  11. Cotton-wool spots are? Nerve-fiber-layer microinfarcts.
