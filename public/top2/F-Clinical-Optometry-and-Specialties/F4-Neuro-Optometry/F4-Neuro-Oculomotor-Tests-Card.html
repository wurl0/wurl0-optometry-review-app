Card02Neuro tests

Every function has its own test

The neuro exam is a matching game: name the function and name the tool. Saccades, accommodation, ocular alignment, sensory fusion, each has a signature test. Learn the pairings and the Hirschberg numbers, and the "which test" questions answer themselves.

01 Oculomotor: saccades, pursuits, fixation

TestMeasures
King-DevickRapid number naming (saccades); losing place while reading, concussion sideline
DEM (Developmental Eye Movement)Visual-verbal saccadic skill, objective; also TBI saccade screen
NSUCO / direct observationPursuit and saccade quality (accuracy, head movement)

02 Accommodation and vergence

RAF rule

The push-up RAF (Royal Air Force) rule measures the amplitude of accommodation and the near point of convergence. A significantly receded near point of accommodation means accommodative insufficiency. Dynamic (MEM) retinoscopy reads the accommodative lag.

03 Ocular alignment (motor)

From quick estimate to gold standard.

TestNote
HirschbergQuick reflex estimate, no prisms; a reflex about 5° nasal is a normal positive angle kappa
KrimskyPrism over the fixing eye to center the reflex
Cover / uncoverTropia (uncover) vs phoria (movement on removal)
Prism cover testThe gold standard: objective, prism-neutralized measurement

04 Sensory and binocular

TestMeasures
Worth 4-dotFusion vs suppression vs diplopia (4 = fuse, 2/3 = suppress, 5 = diplopia)
Maddox rodTurns a point into a line to measure phoria; double Maddox rod for cyclo/torsion
Bagolini / red lensSuppression and retinal correspondence (least dissociating)
Stereo (Titmus, TNO, Randot, Lang)Stereoacuity; Titmus has monocular cues, random-dot removes them
SynoptophoreDiagnoses AND treats; measures fusion ranges and grades

05 The neuro side (quick recap)

Pathway, pupil, palsy

Fields and pupils are on Physiological Optics Card 2: optic nerve = ipsilateral blindness with an absent direct reflex; chiasm = bitemporal; temporal-lobe (Meyer's loop) = superior "pie in the sky"; the blind spot is a negative absolute scotoma; the LGB relays to cortex. The cranial-nerve palsies (down-and-out CN III with the pupil rule, CN IV head tilt, CN VI esotropia) are on Visual Biology Card 4.

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. King-Devick / DEM measure? Saccadic (visual-verbal) eye movements, number-naming; concussion and reading screens.
  2. RAF rule? Amplitude of accommodation and near point of convergence (push-up).
  3. Receded near point of accommodation means? Accommodative insufficiency.
  4. Worth 4-dot results? 4 = fusion, 2 or 3 = suppression, 5 = diplopia.
  5. Maddox rod vs double Maddox rod? Maddox turns a point into a line for phoria; double Maddox measures cyclo/torsion.
  6. Gold standard for measuring deviation? Prism cover test (objective, prism-neutralized).
  7. Hirschberg reflex at pupil margin, mid-iris, limbus? About 15, 30, and 45 degrees (roughly 1 mm = 7 degrees = 15 prism diopters).
  8. Reflex about 5 degrees nasal? Normal positive angle kappa (not a true tropia).
  9. Synoptophore does what? Both diagnoses and treats; measures fusion ranges and BV grades.
  10. Titmus vs random-dot stereo? Titmus (contour) has monocular cues; random-dot (TNO/Randot) removes them.
  11. Optic nerve lesion pupil finding? Ipsilateral blindness with an absent direct light reflex (afferent cut).
  12. Temporal-lobe (Meyer's loop) field defect? Superior homonymous quadrantanopsia (pie in the sky).
