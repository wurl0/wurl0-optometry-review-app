Card04DPAs

Two ways to open a pupil, and the drops that do it

Diagnostic agents dilate, numb, or stain, so you can see and measure. The pupil has two muscles pulling opposite ways, and a drop either drives the opener or releases the closer. Learn the toolkit, the dilation logic, and the one precaution before you dilate.

02 Mydriatics and cycloplegics

AgentClassEffect / use
Phenylephrine 2.5%Sympathomimetic (α)Dilation only, no cycloplegia; add to speed dilation
Tropicamide 0.5-1%Antimuscarinic (short)Fast dilation + light cycloplegia; the routine dilating DPA
Cyclopentolate 1%AntimuscarinicStronger cycloplegia; pediatric refraction (recovers ~24 h)
HomatropineAntimuscarinicIntermediate cycloplegia
AtropineAntimuscarinic (longest)Days-long; dense cycloplegia, accommodative esotropia, uveitis
Two hooks

Phenylephrine dilates but does NOT relax focusing (pure mydriatic). Antimuscarinics do both. Under RA 8050, tropicamide is the authorized diagnostic dilating agent; the fastest full dilation combines phenylephrine + tropicamide.

03 Anesthetics and dyes

AgentUse
Proparacaine, tetracaine, benoxinateTopical anesthesia for tonometry, gonioscopy, foreign-body removal (proparacaine stings least)
FluoresceinEpithelial defects, Seidel test, TBUT, applanation tonometry, contact-lens fitting
Rose bengal / lissamine greenStain devitalized cells and mucus (dry eye)
Never send an anesthetic home

Topical anesthetics are for the chair only. Repeated use is toxic to the epithelium and delays healing, so they are never prescribed for a patient to take away.

04 Before you dilate

Check the angle first

Estimate the anterior chamber angle (Van Herick, or gonioscopy) before instilling a mydriatic. In a narrow angle, dilation can trigger acute angle-closure glaucoma. Document pupils and, for 10% phenylephrine, be cautious in cardiovascular disease (systemic pressor effect).

After dilating

Warn about glare and blurred near vision for hours (longer with cyclopentolate/atropine). Reversal is not routine; if needed, a weak miotic can be used, but usually you just let it wear off.

07 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. The two iris muscles and their supply? Radial dilator (sympathetic, alpha-1) opens; circular sphincter (parasympathetic, M3) closes.
  2. Two ways a drop dilates? Stimulate the dilator (phenylephrine) or block the sphincter (antimuscarinic).
  3. Phenylephrine effect? Mydriasis only, no cycloplegia (pure alpha agonist).
  4. Routine dilating DPA (and PH-authorized)? Tropicamide (fast dilation + light cycloplegia).
  5. Strong pediatric cycloplegic? Cyclopentolate (atropine for the densest).
  6. Longest-acting cycloplegic? Atropine (days).
  7. Fastest full dilation combo? Phenylephrine + tropicamide.
  8. Topical anesthetics? Proparacaine (stings least), tetracaine, benoxinate; chair use only, never prescribed.
  9. Fluorescein uses? Epithelial defects, Seidel, TBUT, applanation, CL fitting.
  10. Rose bengal / lissamine green stain? Devitalized cells and mucus (dry eye).
  11. What to check before dilating? The anterior chamber angle (Van Herick); narrow angle risks acute angle closure.
  12. Caution with 10% phenylephrine? Systemic pressor effect, cautious in cardiovascular disease.
