Card03Pediatric

The eye grows toward emmetropia and 20/20

A newborn is hyperopic and nearly blind by adult standards, then the eye tunes itself and vision sharpens on a schedule. Learn the milestones, the age-matched acuity tests, and why kids need cycloplegia, and the pediatric block falls into place.

01 Visual development, on a timeline

Refraction starts hyperopic and drifts toward emmetropia (emmetropization), while acuity climbs from about 20/400 at birth to 20/20 by the preschool years.

02 Testing acuity by age

Age / abilityMethod
Preverbal infantFixation and follow, CSM, and preferential looking (Teller / Cardiff cards)
Toddler / preschoolMatching optotypes: Lea symbols, HOTV, Broken Wheel, Allen pictures
School ageLetter charts (Snellen, ETDRS)
Match the test to the child

A cooperative 3-year-old who cannot read letters is tested with Lea symbols (or HOTV matching), not a Snellen chart. Objective retinoscopy works at any age and carries the infant refraction.

03 Why cycloplegia

Kids have huge, active accommodation

A child can mask hyperopia (latent) and over-minus themselves by accommodating during the exam. Cycloplegic refraction relaxes it to reveal the true error. Cyclopentolate is the usual agent; atropine is reserved for dense cycloplegia and accommodative esotropia.

Accommodative esotropia

An uncorrected hyperope over-accommodates and over-converges, turning eso. The fix is the full plus (cycloplegic) prescription, sometimes with a near add for a high AC/A.

04 Screening: catch amblyopia and the dangerous ones

The quick pediatric battery

Red reflex / Bruckner (asymmetry flags strabismus, anisometropia, or media opacity), Hirschberg and cover test for alignment, and stereopsis for binocularity. Detect amblyopia early, in the critical period, for the best result.

Leukocoria is a referral, now

A white pupil (leukocoria) or an absent red reflex means urgent referral, to rule out retinoblastoma and congenital cataract. This is the pediatric emergency to never miss.

07 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Newborn refraction and acuity? Hyperopic, about 20/400.
  2. Emmetropization? The eye drifts from hyperopia toward emmetropia over early childhood.
  3. When does stereopsis appear? Around 3 to 6 months.
  4. Adult-level 20/20 by what age? About 5 to 6 years (the fovea matures over the first years).
  5. Preverbal acuity test? Preferential looking (Teller/Cardiff), plus fixation-and-follow / CSM.
  6. Cooperative 3-year-old who cannot read letters? Lea symbols (or HOTV matching).
  7. Why cycloplegic refraction in kids? To relax strong accommodation and reveal latent hyperopia (avoid over-minus).
  8. Cycloplegic agents? Cyclopentolate usually; atropine for dense cycloplegia / accommodative esotropia.
  9. Accommodative esotropia management? Full plus (cycloplegic) prescription, sometimes a near add for high AC/A.
  10. Red reflex / Bruckner screens for? Strabismus, anisometropia, and media opacities (asymmetry is the flag).
  11. Leukocoria means? Urgent referral to rule out retinoblastoma and congenital cataract.
  12. Why treat amblyopia early? Best results within the critical period of visual development.
