Card06Embryology

Ocular embryology: which layer built what

Almost every embryology question is one question: which germ tissue did this structure come from. Learn that table, then the optic-cup story that ties it together, and the classic anomalies fall out as development gone wrong. This closes the Visual Biology set.

01 The four sources

Four embryonic tissues build the eye. The one that trips people: the lens is surface ectoderm, and neural crest makes most of the connective tissue.

Germ layerOcular structures
Surface ectodermLens; corneal, conjunctival and eyelid epithelium; lacrimal and lid glands
Neuroectoderm (optic cup)Neural retina + RPE, optic nerve, iris and ciliary epithelium, iris sphincter and dilator muscles
Neural crest (mesectoderm)Corneal stroma and endothelium, sclera, trabecular meshwork, ciliary muscle, iris stroma, choroid, orbital bones
MesodermExtraocular muscles, vascular endothelium, Schlemm's canal endothelium
Two hooks

Corneal epithelium is surface ectoderm, but its stroma and endothelium are neural crest, same cornea, two origins. And the iris muscles are neuroectoderm, a rare case of muscle from ectoderm; the extraocular muscles are ordinary mesoderm.

02 The optic-cup story

The eye grows out of the forebrain. A vesicle balloons out, touches the skin, and folds in on itself, and that fold becomes the whole back of the eye.

03 The leftovers

Hyaloid remnants and myelin

The hyaloid artery feeds the fetal lens, then regresses. What it can leave behind: a Mittendorf dot on the back of the lens, a Bergmeister papilla on the disc, or, if it fails to regress, persistent fetal vasculature (PHPV) with leukocoria. Separately, optic nerve myelination travels from the brain toward the eye and stops at the lamina cribrosa, an overshoot leaves myelinated retinal nerve fibers.

07 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. What does the lens come from? Surface ectoderm.
  2. Corneal epithelium vs stroma and endothelium origin? Epithelium from surface ectoderm; stroma and endothelium from neural crest.
  3. Origin of the neural retina AND the RPE? Neuroectoderm, the inner and outer walls of the optic cup.
  4. Origin of the extraocular muscles? Mesoderm.
  5. Origin of the iris sphincter and dilator muscles? Neuroectoderm (unusual muscle from ectoderm).
  6. What does neural crest supply in the eye? Most connective tissue: corneal stroma/endothelium, sclera, trabecular meshwork, ciliary muscle, iris stroma, choroid.
  7. What do the optic-cup walls become? Inner wall neural retina, outer wall RPE; the space between is the detachment plane.
  8. Coloboma is a failure of? Closure of the choroid (optic) fissure; the defect is inferonasal.
  9. What induces the lens placode? The optic vesicle contacting the surface ectoderm.
  10. Three hyaloid artery remnants? Mittendorf dot, Bergmeister papilla, persistent fetal vasculature (PHPV).
  11. Where does optic nerve myelination stop? At the lamina cribrosa (overshoot = myelinated nerve fibers).
  12. First-trimester rubella eye effects? Cataract, salt-and-pepper retinopathy, glaucoma.
