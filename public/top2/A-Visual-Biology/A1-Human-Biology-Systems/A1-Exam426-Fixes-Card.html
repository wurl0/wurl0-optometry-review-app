03 Cluster 2 · A3 embryology (the 69%)

Two ideas carry almost all ten misses: the three vitreous generations, and the surface-ectoderm vs neural-crest split. Get these two pictures and A3 stops being guesswork.

Surface ectoderm

The outside-in list

  • Lens
  • Corneal epithelium
  • Lacrimal gland
  • Conjunctival epithelium
Neural crest

The stroma / support list

  • Corneal stroma + endothelium
  • Most of the sclera, choroidal stroma
  • Ciliary muscle, iris stroma
  • Trabecular meshwork
Hook

Lens sits on the surface, so lens and the epithelia are surface ectoderm. Crest builds the scaffolding: stroma, endothelium, meshwork, muscle. If a structure is a clear covering layer, think surface ectoderm; if it is connective or support tissue, think neural crest.

The two that caught you

Corneal stroma and endothelium are neural crest, not ectoderm (only the epithelium is ectoderm). And the fetal fissure closes at week 7: fail to close and you get an inferonasal coloboma. Anchor the direction: colobomas point down and in.

Distinguish these two

Mittendorf dot = benign hyaloid remnant on the back of the lens. PHPV/PFV = persistent primary vitreous, a retrolental fibrovascular mass, and the board key is no calcium, which is what separates it from retinoblastoma.

Why the split matters clinically

A central retinal artery occlusion whites out the inner retina but the fovea stays red, the cherry-red spot, because its outer layers still get choroidal blood. That single fact ties the anatomy to the disease you will see tested in Subject E.

07 Spaced-repetition deck

Drop these into your FoqusLab queue and answer out loud before you flip.

  1. First antibody in a response vs most abundant in serum? IgM first; IgG most abundant.
  2. Antibody in tears and colostrum? IgA (secretory).
  3. PTH vs calcitonin on blood calcium? PTH raises it, calcitonin lowers it.
  4. CN VII exit, and the thinnest motor nerve? Stylomastoid foramen; CN IV is thinnest.
  5. The three vitreous generations and what each becomes? Primary (vascular, regresses), secondary (adult gel), tertiary (lens zonules).
  6. Corneal stroma and endothelium come from? Neural crest; only the epithelium is surface ectoderm.
  7. Lens, lacrimal gland origin? Surface ectoderm.
  8. Fetal fissure closes when, failure gives? Week 7; inferonasal coloboma.
  9. PHPV vs retinoblastoma, the key sign? PHPV has no calcium; retinoblastoma calcifies.
  10. Adie pupil confirming test? Dilute pilocarpine 0.125% constricts it (denervation supersensitivity).
  11. Argyll-Robertson pattern? Small, irregular, light absent, near preserved; neurosyphilis.
  12. Meyer loop lesion field defect? Contralateral superior quadrantanopia (pie in the sky).
  13. LGN uncrossed vs crossed layers? Uncrossed 2,3,5; crossed 1,4,6.
  14. Inner vs outer retina blood supply? CRA inner; choriocapillaris outer.
  15. Macula and foveola diameters? Macula 4.5 mm; foveola 0.4 mm.
