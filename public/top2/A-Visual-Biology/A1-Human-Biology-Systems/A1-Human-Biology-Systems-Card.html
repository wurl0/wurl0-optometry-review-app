Card07A1 systems

Human biology systems: the general A&P the eye exam hides behind

The MCU "Visual Biology" preboard was almost all general human anatomy and physiology. Of 100 items, one touched the eye. The misses were scattered across every body system, so this is a breadth problem, not a few bad ideas. This card pins down the exact facts you dropped, groups them by system, and pulls out the swap-pairs first because they are the fastest points back.

01 Read this first: it is a coverage problem

Unlike pharma, where the misses clustered, here they are spread evenly: blood, kidney, gut, endocrine, reproductive, heart, larynx, bone. That pattern means the A1 general-biology base is thin across the board, not broken in one place.

Honest framing

This card is the entry point, not the whole fix. It covers the actual preboard misses. The real lever for this subject is a systematic re-drill of the A1 human-biology material, one system at a time. Do not let this one card stand in for coverage.

04 The heart

Your biggest cluster. The conduction path, one auscultation point, and two definitions carried most of the misses.

The exact misses

Pacemaker = SA node (you put AV). Force of contraction = inotropic (you put chronotropic, which is rate). QRS = ventricular depolarization. Fetal vessel joining pulmonary artery to aorta = ductus arteriosus (foramen ovale joins the atria). Orthopnea points to CHF.

The nasolacrimal duct opening at the inferior meatus is the one eye-relevant fact in this whole exam. It reappears in lacrimal drainage and in dye-disappearance / Jones testing. Bank it in both places.

The spelling trap that cost a point

ileum is the last part of the small intestine; ilium is the flat bone of the pelvis. The "not a pelvic bone" answer is the one spelled with an e: ileum.

08 Rebuild it on paper

This subject is anatomy-heavy, so lean on diagrams and lists, not just term recall. Close the card and do these blind.

1 · Brain dump the swap-pairs

Write all seven pairs with both members and the separator phrase. Then one high-yield fact per body system from memory.

2 · Draw the heart

Sketch it and label SA node, AV node, the four valves, and the mitral apex point. Then write the conduction path in order.

3 · Draw the top cervical spine

Draw C1, C2, C7 and label Atlas, Axis (dens), and vertebra prominens. No prompts.

4 · Teach the blood types

Explain, without naming the cells first, why O donates to all but receives only from O, and why AB is the opposite.

5 · List the paranasal sinuses

Write the four (frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid, maxillary) and cross out the distractor (mastoid).

09 Spaced-repetition deck

Drop these into your FoqusLab review queue on the 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Monocyte in tissue vs basophil in tissue? Macrophage; mast cell.
  2. Alpha cells vs beta cells of the islets? Glucagon; insulin.
  3. Pancreatic protein enzyme vs carb enzyme? Chymotrypsin; amylopsin.
  4. Universal donor vs universal recipient? O (no antigens); AB (no antibodies).
  5. C1 and C2 names? Atlas (C1); Axis (C2, has the dens).
  6. Hemoglobin carrying CO2 vs holding ferric iron? Carbaminohemoglobin; methemoglobin.
  7. B-lymphocytes make which immunity? Humoral.
  8. Pacemaker of the heart, and what QRS shows? SA node; ventricular depolarization.
  9. Force vs rate of contraction (the -tropic words)? Inotropic = force; chronotropic = rate.
  10. Where does the mitral valve get auscultated? 5th ICS, left mid-clavicular line (apex).
  11. Where does the nasolacrimal duct drain? Inferior meatus.
  12. "Not a pelvic bone": ileum or ilium? Ileum (the intestine); ilium is the pelvic bone.
