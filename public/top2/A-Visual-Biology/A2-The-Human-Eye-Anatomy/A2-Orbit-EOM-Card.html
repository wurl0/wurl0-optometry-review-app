Card04Orbit & muscles

Six muscles, three nerves: read the H

Every eye-movement question is the same picture: six muscles on three cranial nerves, tested one at a time in the six diagnostic positions. Learn the field-of-action H, the two rules that decide the obliques, and the three palsies, and the rest is recognition.

01 The field-of-action H

To isolate a muscle, move the eye into the position where that muscle does its cleanest work. For the right eye, here is who turns it where.

02 Muscle, nerve, action

The innervation rule

LR6, SO4, the rest is 3. Lateral rectus on CN VI, superior oblique on CN IV, and everything else (MR, SR, IR, IO plus the levator and the pupil) on CN III.

03 The two rules that decide the verticals

Recti work best abducted

Turn the eye out about 23 degrees and the superior and inferior recti line up with the visual axis, giving clean elevation and depression. That is why you test SR up-and-out and IR down-and-out.

Obliques work best adducted

The obliques do their vertical work when the eye is turned in. The one the board hammers: the superior oblique depresses the adducted eye (down and in, the reading and stair-descending direction). The inferior oblique elevates the adducted eye.

Torsion hook

The two superior muscles intort (SR, SO). The two inferior muscles extort (IR, IO). Superior in, inferior out.

04 The three palsies

CN III, and the pupil question

The eye sits down and out (only LR and SO still pull), with ptosis. Now the pupil decides the cause: a blown, fixed pupil means a compressive lesion (aneurysm), a surgical emergency, because the pupil fibers ride on the outside of the nerve. A pupil-sparing third-nerve palsy is usually ischemic (diabetic, microvascular).

CN IV (superior oblique)

Vertical diplopia that is worst looking down and in (reading, going downstairs). The patient tilts the head away from the weak side, and the Bielschowsky head-tilt test is positive. It is the longest, thinnest cranial nerve and the only one to exit dorsally.

CN VI (lateral rectus)

Esotropia, the eye turned in and unable to abduct, with horizontal diplopia worse at distance. Its long intracranial course makes it a false-localizing sign in raised intracranial pressure.

05 The orbit

Bones and openings

Seven bones form the orbit. The optic canal carries CN II and the ophthalmic artery; the superior orbital fissure carries CN III, IV, V1, VI and the superior ophthalmic vein; the inferior orbital fissure carries V2. The four recti all originate at the annulus of Zinn.

Blowout fractures

The floor (maxilla) and medial wall (lamina papyracea of the ethmoid) are the thinnest. A floor blowout can entrap the inferior rectus, so the eye cannot look up, with vertical diplopia and numbness over the cheek (infraorbital nerve).

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. The EOM innervation rule? LR6 (lateral rectus, CN VI), SO4 (superior oblique, CN IV), all others CN III.
  2. Primary actions of the four recti? MR adduct, LR abduct, SR elevate, IR depress.
  3. Primary actions of the two obliques? Superior oblique intorts, inferior oblique extorts.
  4. Where do SR/IR act best, and the obliques? SR and IR best in abduction; the obliques best in adduction.
  5. What does the superior oblique do to the adducted eye? Depresses it (down and in).
  6. Intorters and extorters? Intorters: SR + SO (the superiors). Extorters: IR + IO (the inferiors).
  7. Yoke muscles for right gaze and for up-and-right? Right gaze: right LR + left MR. Up-and-right: right SR + left IO.
  8. CN III palsy picture and the pupil rule? Eye down and out with ptosis; a blown pupil means compressive (aneurysm, surgical), pupil-sparing is usually ischemic.
  9. CN IV palsy features? Vertical diplopia worse looking down and in, head tilt away from the weak side, positive Bielschowsky.
  10. CN VI palsy features? Esotropia, cannot abduct, horizontal diplopia worse at distance.
  11. Optic canal vs superior orbital fissure contents? Optic canal: CN II + ophthalmic artery. SOF: CN III, IV, V1, VI + superior ophthalmic vein.
  12. Orbital floor blowout consequence? Inferior rectus entrapment, limited upgaze, vertical diplopia, infraorbital numbness.
