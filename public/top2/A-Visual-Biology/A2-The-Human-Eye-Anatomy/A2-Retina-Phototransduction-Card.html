Card02The retina

The retina: ten layers, and the switch that runs backward

Light enters at the front and has to travel through nine layers before it reaches the photoreceptors at the very back. Learn the stack in order, then the one cascade that turns a photon into a signal, and notice that the switch fires the opposite way you expect.

01 The ten layers, inner to outer

The retina is inverted: light passes the inner layers first and the photoreceptors sit last, against the pigment epithelium. Read the stack from the vitreous side down.

03 Phototransduction: a photon becomes silence

This is the one cascade to own, and the trick is that light turns the photoreceptor off.

The cascade

A photon isomerizes 11-cis retinal to all-trans, which activates rhodopsin, then transducin (a G protein), then phosphodiesterase. PDE chews up cGMP, so cGMP falls, the cGMP-gated sodium channels close, and the photoreceptor hyperpolarizes and releases less glutamate.photon → 11-cis to all-trans retinal → rhodopsin → transducin → PDE → cGMP down → Na channels close → hyperpolarize → less glutamate

The switch runs backward

In the dark, cGMP is high, the sodium channels are open, and a steady dark current keeps the photoreceptor depolarized and releasing glutamate. Light shuts that down. So unlike most neurons, a photoreceptor hyperpolarizes to its stimulus and signals by releasing less transmitter.

Recovery and vitamin A

All-trans retinal shuttles to the RPE, is rebuilt into 11-cis retinal, and returns, the visual cycle. Vitamin A feeds it, which is why deficiency causes night blindness before anything else.

05 The special spots and the blood

Fovea and blind spot

The fovea is cones only, no rods, with the inner layers swept aside so light reaches the cones cleanly, giving the sharpest vision. Its avascular centre is fed from behind by the choroid. The optic disc has no photoreceptors at all, that is the blind spot.

Two blood supplies, one exam favorite

The inner retina runs on the central retinal artery; the outer retina (photoreceptors and RPE) runs on the choriocapillaris. That split is why a central retinal artery occlusion whitens the inner retina yet leaves a cherry-red spot, the fovea still glowing on its intact choroidal supply.

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Ten layers inner to outer? ILM, NFL, GCL, IPL, INL, OPL, ONL, ELM, photoreceptors, RPE.
  2. Which layer holds photoreceptor nuclei? The outer nuclear layer.
  3. Which cells sit in the inner nuclear layer? Bipolar, horizontal, amacrine, and Muller cells.
  4. Do photoreceptors depolarize or hyperpolarize to light? Hyperpolarize, releasing less glutamate.
  5. The dark current? In darkness, high cGMP keeps sodium channels open, the photoreceptor depolarized and releasing glutamate.
  6. Phototransduction cascade order? 11-cis to all-trans retinal, rhodopsin, transducin, PDE, cGMP falls, Na channels close, hyperpolarize.
  7. RPE functions? Phagocytose outer segments, recycle vitamin A (visual cycle), absorb stray light, outer blood-retinal barrier.
  8. Rods vs cones in one line? Rods scotopic, peripheral, rhodopsin, no colour; cones photopic, central, colour, high acuity.
  9. Fovea specialization? Cones only, no rods, inner layers displaced, avascular centre fed by the choroid.
  10. Retinal blood-supply split? Inner retina from the central retinal artery; outer retina (photoreceptors, RPE) from the choriocapillaris.
  11. Vitamin A deficiency causes? Night blindness (nyctalopia), then xerophthalmia.
  12. Principal retinal glia and what it forms? Muller cells; they form the ELM and ILM.
