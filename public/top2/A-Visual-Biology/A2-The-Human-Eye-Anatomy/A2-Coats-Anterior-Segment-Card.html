Card03Coats & media

Three coats and the clear media: build the globe

The eye is three shells around three clear media. Get the shells in order, then the cornea layer by layer with the two that never come back, and the anterior chamber stops being a list of parts. This is the anatomy the board leans on hardest.

01 The three coats

From outside in, three tunics wrap the eye, each with a different job.

CoatStructuresJob
Outer, fibrousCornea + scleraShape, protection, refraction
Middle, vascular (uvea)Iris + ciliary body + choroidNutrition, IOP, accommodation, light control
Inner, neuralRetinaPhototransduction (Card 2)

02 The cornea, five layers

Front to back, five layers. The one thing to lock: two of them never regenerate, so damage there scars or swells for good.

Why the cornea is clear, and so strong a lens

Transparency needs four things: regular collagen spacing, no blood vessels, deturgescence (the endothelial pump keeps it slightly dehydrated), and no myelin on its nerves. It is the eye's most powerful refracting surface (about +43 D, two-thirds of the total), and its sensation runs on CN V1. Lose the endothelial pump and the stroma floods, corneal edema and bullous keratopathy.

03 The sclera

Same collagen, different order

The sclera is the cornea's opaque continuation: the same collagen but irregularly arranged, which scatters light and looks white. It is thinnest under the rectus insertions and at the lamina cribrosa, where the optic nerve exits, the weak point that cups in glaucoma. It meets the cornea at the limbus, home of the corneal stem cells.

04 The uvea: the vascular middle

Choroid layers, outer to inner

Haller's (large vessels) → Sattler's (medium vessels) → choriocapillaris (the capillary bed that feeds the RPE), all resting on Bruch's membrane. The iris muscles here are the Ocular Pharma Card 1 wiring, and the aqueous the ciliary body makes is the Card 4 glaucoma story.

05 The three clear media

MediumMade / natureBoard facts
Aqueous humorCiliary processes; plasma-like, low protein, high ascorbateFills both chambers; drains trabecular meshwork → Schlemm's canal (+ uveoscleral); feeds cornea and lens; IOP ~10-21
LensBiconvex, avascular, no nerves; grows for lifeCapsule = thickest basement membrane in the body; crystallins; changes shape to accommodate; cataract = opacity
VitreousGel, 99% water + collagen + hyaluronanFills ~4/5 of the globe; base at the ora serrata = firmest attachment; Cloquet's canal; ages by syneresis → PVD, floaters
Why avascular structures rely on aqueous

The cornea and lens have to stay crystal clear, so they carry no blood vessels. That leaves the aqueous humor as their food and oxygen supply, which is exactly why a healthy anterior chamber matters to both, and why the vitreous, also avascular, degenerates with age instead of healing.

06 Cross-links

Say these out loud

Card 2: the choriocapillaris you just met is what feeds the photoreceptors and RPE. Ocular Pharma Card 1 and 4: the iris muscles and the aqueous production-and-drainage loop are the pupil and glaucoma pharmacology. Ocular Disease: keratoconus and corneal dystrophies, uveitis, cataract, and posterior vitreous detachment all live in these structures. Primary Eye Care: gonioscopy of the angle, pachymetry, and the slit-lamp exam read this anatomy directly.

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Three coats and their components? Outer fibrous (cornea + sclera), middle vascular / uvea (iris, ciliary body, choroid), inner neural (retina).
  2. Five corneal layers front to back? Epithelium, Bowman's, stroma, Descemet's, endothelium.
  3. Which corneal layers do NOT regenerate? Bowman's (scars) and endothelium (no division); epithelium and Descemet's do.
  4. What keeps the cornea clear, and what if it fails? The endothelial pump (deturgescence); failure gives corneal edema / bullous keratopathy.
  5. Corneal sensation nerve and refractive power? CN V1 (nasociliary); about +43 D, two-thirds of the eye's power.
  6. Aqueous: made where, drained where? Made by the ciliary processes; drained via the trabecular meshwork into Schlemm's canal (plus uveoscleral).
  7. Choroid layers outer to inner? Haller's (large vessels), Sattler's (medium), choriocapillaris (feeds RPE), on Bruch's membrane.
  8. Lens facts: nutrition, growth, capsule? Avascular, fed by aqueous, grows throughout life, capsule is the thickest basement membrane.
  9. Firmest vitreous attachment? The vitreous base at the ora serrata.
  10. Three parts of the uvea and one job each? Iris (light), ciliary body (aqueous + accommodation), choroid (nourish outer retina).
  11. Why is the cornea transparent? Regular collagen spacing, avascularity, deturgescence (endothelial pump), no myelin.
  12. Weakest point of the sclera? The lamina cribrosa (optic nerve exit), where glaucoma cups the disc.
