Card06Accommodation & AC/A

Focus and converge, locked together

Look at something close and three things fire at once, and focus drags convergence along for the ride. Learn the near triad, how AC/A ties the two, and the phoria rules, and every accommodative and vergence diagnosis falls out of two numbers.

01 The near triad, and how the lens focuses

Fixate a near target and the near reflex fires three responses together, all parasympathetic on CN III: accommodation, convergence, and miosis (pupil constriction).

Amplitude falls with age (Hofstetter)

Min = 15 − 0.25 × age   Avg = 18.5 − 0.3 × age   Max = 25 − 0.4 × age. Each part: the constant is the young maximum, the age term is the yearly loss of lens elasticity. When amplitude no longer covers the near demand, that is presbyopia, and you add plus.

02 AC/A: focus pulls convergence

Accommodating a target automatically drives some convergence, the accommodative convergence. The AC/A ratio is how many prism diopters of convergence come per diopter of accommodation. Normal is about 4:1 (roughly 3 to 5).

Two ways to get the number

Calculated: AC/A = IPD + m(Δn − Δd). IPD in cm, m = near test distance in meters (e.g. 0.4), and phoria Δ is signed (eso = +, exo = −). Gradient: add a known lens, remeasure the phoria, and AC/A = (Δwith − Δwithout) / lens power. The gradient holds the target fixed, so it excludes proximal convergence.

High vs low, and what it does at near

High AC/A: a little focusing over-converges the eyes, so the patient goes eso at near. Low AC/A: focusing barely converges, so the patient goes exo at near. The ratio predicts the deviation.

03 Phorias and the vergence anomalies

A phoria is a latent misalignment held straight by fusion; a tropia is a manifest one. Break the anomalies down by where the deviation is worse and the AC/A.

04 Will the phoria be comfortable? Two criteria

Sheard's criterion (best for EXO)

The compensating fusional reserve should be at least twice the phoria. So for a 6Δ exophoria you need at least 12Δ of opposing (base-out / positive) fusional vergence to be comfortable. Fall short and prescribe prism = (2 × phoria − reserve) / 3.

Percival's criterion (best for ESO)

Keep the fixation demand in the middle third of the range of clear single binocular vision. It looks only at the vergence range, ignoring the phoria itself, which is why it suits eso deviations where Sheard misbehaves.

Pairing hook

Sheard for exophoria, Percival for esophoria. Both ask the same real question: is enough fusional reserve left over to hold the eyes straight without strain?

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. The near triad? Accommodation, convergence, and miosis, all parasympathetic on CN III.
  2. How does the lens accommodate (Helmholtz)? Ciliary muscle contracts, zonules slacken, the elastic lens rounds up to add plus.
  3. Hofstetter average amplitude? 18.5 minus 0.3 times age (min = 15 - 0.25 age, max = 25 - 0.4 age).
  4. Normal AC/A ratio? About 4:1 (roughly 3 to 5 prism diopters per diopter).
  5. Calculated vs gradient AC/A? Calculated = IPD + m(phoria near - phoria dist); gradient = phoria change per diopter of added lens (excludes proximal convergence).
  6. High AC/A does what at near? Over-converges, so eso at near (convergence excess).
  7. Low AC/A does what at near? Under-converges, so exo at near (convergence insufficiency).
  8. Phoria vs tropia? Phoria = latent, held straight by fusion; tropia = manifest.
  9. Convergence insufficiency signs? Exo greater at near, receded NPC, low AC/A, fails Sheard; near asthenopia.
  10. Sheard's criterion? Compensating fusional reserve should be at least twice the phoria (best for exophoria).
  11. Percival's criterion? Keep the demand in the middle third of the clear-single-BV range; ignores the phoria (best for esophoria).
  12. Accommodative excess vs insufficiency? Excess = over-focuses (lead, pseudomyopia, hard to relax); insufficiency = amplitude below norm (near blur, receded NPA, high lag).
