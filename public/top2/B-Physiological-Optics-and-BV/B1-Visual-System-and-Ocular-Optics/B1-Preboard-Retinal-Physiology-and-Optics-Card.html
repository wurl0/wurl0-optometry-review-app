Card07Retinal physiology

Retinal physiology, optics, and the field defects

The physiology of phototransduction carried most of the misses, followed by a set of physio-optics facts and the neuro field-defect localizations. Learn the dark-current story once and a dozen questions answer themselves.

01 Phototransduction: the dark current, told forward

In the dark, cGMP holds the sodium channels open, so the photoreceptor is depolarized and steadily releases glutamate. Light triggers the cascade that shuts this off.

The counterintuitive one

Light makes the photoreceptor hyperpolarize and release less glutamate, the opposite of a typical neuron. The outer retina (photoreceptors) is fed by the choriocapillaris; the inner retina by the central retinal artery.

Anchors

Blue bends most, so it focuses first (shortest focal length): that is chromatic aberration. Magno = motion and low contrast (big, fast, colorblind); parvo = fine detail and color. UV-A is the longest UV wavelength, so it goes deepest.

Homonymous points to the opposite side

A homonymous defect is retrochiasmal and opposite the lesion: right radiation gives a left field loss. A Wernicke (hemianopic) pupil localizes the defect to the optic tract, where the pupil fibers still run.

06 Spaced-repetition deck

  4. Regenerates 11-cis retinal? Retinal isomerase.
  5. Outer retina's blood supply? Choriocapillaris.
  6. Largest, fastest ganglion cells? Y (magnocellular) cells.
  7. Which focuses in front, blue or red? Blue (shorter focal length).
  8. Depth of focus increases with? A smaller pupil.
  9. Main factor in retinal image size? Axial length.
  10. Right optic radiation lesion gives? Left homonymous hemianopsia.
  11. Fixed dilated unilateral pupil from compression? Hutchinson's pupil.
