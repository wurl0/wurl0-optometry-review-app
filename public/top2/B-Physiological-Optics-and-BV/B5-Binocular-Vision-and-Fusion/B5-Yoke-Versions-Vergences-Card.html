Card01Versions & vergences

Both eyes, one command: yoke, version, vergence

Card 4 of Visual Biology gave you the six muscles of one eye. This is the layer above it: how the brain drives the pair. Nail two laws, one yoke chart, and the naming decoder, and every version, vergence, and diplopia question turns into recognition.

01 Two ways the eyes move

Every binocular movement is one of two kinds, decided by whether the eyes move the same way or opposite ways.

Version = conjugate (same direction)

Both eyes swing the same way, like a soldier's eyes tracking a passing car. Look right = dextroversion, look up = supraversion (sursumversion). The pair stays parallel.

Vergence = disjunctive (opposite directions)

The eyes move toward or away from each other. Both turn in for a near target = convergence; both turn out = divergence. This is the only time the two visual axes are not parallel.

Hook

Version = eyes go the very same way. Vergence = eyes diverge/converge, they disagree.

02 The two laws that govern the pair

Hering's law of equal innervation (between the two eyes)

Yoke muscles of the two eyes get an equal and simultaneous stream of innervation. Push both eyes right and the right lateral rectus and left medial rectus fire together, in equal measure. This is why a palsy in one eye throws off the other: overdrive to the weak muscle spills equally to its yoke (the basis of primary vs secondary deviation).

Sherrington's law of reciprocal innervation (within one eye)

When a muscle contracts, its antagonist in the same eye relaxes. Right eye adducts: right medial rectus contracts as the right lateral rectus lets go, in equal measure.

Keep them straight

Hering = two eyes (yoke pairs, equal innervation). Sherrington = one eye (agonist vs antagonist, reciprocal). Two names, two scopes.

03 The yoke chart

A yoke pair is the two muscles, one in each eye, that move the eyes together into a given direction of gaze. Drawn from the patient's view (their right is on the right).

How to rebuild it without memorizing 6 pairs

For any diagonal, the abducting eye (the one turning out) uses its rectus: up-and-out = SR, down-and-out = IR. The adducting eye (turning in) uses the oblique that matches the height: up-and-in = IO, down-and-in = SO. So up-and-right: right eye abducts (RSR), left eye adducts up (LIO). Build any box from that one rule.

04 The naming decoder

Board stems hide the movement inside a compound word. Split it: side + vertical + version.

Worked example (a classic stem)

"Yoke muscles are the right superior rectus and left inferior oblique." Decode: RSR abducts-and-elevates the right eye, LIO adducts-and-elevates the left, so both eyes go up and to the right = dextroelevation (dextrosupraversion).

05 Cycloversions and cyclovergences

The eyes can also roll about the line of sight. Same naming logic: same direction = version, mirror = vergence.

Cycloversion (same way)

Both upper corneal poles roll the same direction. Dextrocycloversion = both tops roll to the right, so the right eye extorts (RIO) and the left intorts (LSO).

Cyclovergence (mirror)

The poles roll toward or away from each other. Both tops toward the nose = conclination (incyclovergence); both away = disclination (excyclovergence). Watch the trap: "both eyes rotate inward" is a vergence, not a version.

Torsion hook (carried from Visual Biology Card 4)

Superiors intort (SR, SO), inferiors extort (IR, IO). That decides which muscle rolls which way.

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Version vs vergence? Version = both eyes same direction (conjugate); vergence = opposite directions (disjunctive, con/divergence).
  2. Hering's law? Equal and simultaneous innervation to the yoke muscles of the TWO eyes.
  3. Sherrington's law? Within ONE eye, a muscle contracts as its antagonist relaxes (reciprocal innervation).
  4. Which law is one eye vs two eyes? Sherrington = one eye (agonist/antagonist); Hering = two eyes (yoke pairs).
  5. Yoke pair for dextroversion (look right)? Right lateral rectus + left medial rectus.
  6. Yoke pair for up-and-right (dextroelevation)? Right superior rectus + left inferior oblique.
  7. Yoke pair for down-and-right (dextrodepression)? Right inferior rectus + left superior oblique.
  8. Yoke pair for down-and-left (levodepression)? Right superior oblique + left inferior rectus.
  9. The build-it rule for any yoke box? Abducting eye uses its rectus (up=SR, down=IR); adducting eye uses the matching oblique (up=IO, down=SO).
  10. Dextrocycloversion muscles? Both tops roll right: right eye extorts (RIO), left eye intorts (LSO).
  11. Conclination? Incyclovergence: both upper poles roll toward the nose (a vergence, not a version).
  12. Why does one-eye palsy disturb the other eye? Hering's law: extra innervation to the weak muscle goes equally to its yoke, so the sound eye overshoots (secondary deviation).
