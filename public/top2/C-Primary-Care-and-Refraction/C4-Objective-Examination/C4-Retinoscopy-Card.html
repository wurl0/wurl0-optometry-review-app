Card02Retinoscopy

Read the reflex, chase it to neutral

Retinoscopy is one question asked with light: is the eye's far point in front of your peephole or behind it? The reflex tells you which way, you add lenses until it stops moving, then subtract your working distance. No answers needed from the patient, which is why it carries the pediatric and non-verbal exam.

01 With, against, neutral

Sweep the streak across the pupil and watch the bright reflex. Which way it moves relative to your sweep names the error.

Why it works

You neutralize when the eye's far point sits at your peephole. WITH motion means the far point is behind you (hyperopia, or emmetropia and low myopia less than your working-distance value), so add plus. AGAINST means the far point is between you and the patient (myopia stronger than your working distance), so add minus.

02 Subtract your working distance

Gross minus working distance = net

You sit at a fixed distance, so neutral is reached with extra plus that only cancels that gap. Net = gross − working-distance power. At 67 cm subtract 1.50 D; at 50 cm subtract 2.00 D; at 40 cm subtract 2.50 D.

Worked example

Gross neutral is +2.50 at a 50 cm working distance. Net = +2.50 − 2.00 = +0.50 D.

Hook

Closer to done = fast, bright, wide. A dull, slow, narrow reflex means you are far off, classically a high hyperope in a dim room.

04 Astigmatism on the streak

The tells

In astigmatism the reflex behaves differently in the two meridians. Look for break (the streak offsets as it crosses the axis), skew (reflex and streak rotate against each other off-axis), width and thickness differences, and speed differences between meridians.

Working it

Align the streak with a principal meridian (no break), neutralize it with sphere, rotate 90 degrees and neutralize the second meridian with cylinder. To scope the vertical meridian, the streak is oriented vertically and swept across it.

05 Static vs dynamic

Static (the refraction)

Patient fixates a distance target with accommodation relaxed, and you measure the refractive error. If fixation drifts to near, accommodation creeps in and the result is falsely over-minused, one reason retinoscopy suits non-verbal, pediatric, and geriatric patients who cannot hold subjective responses.

Dynamic (the accommodative response)

Patient fixates a near target. MEM and Nott retinoscopy measure the lag: a with motion means the eye under-accommodates (a lag), neutralized by adding plus. Normal lag is about +0.25 to +0.75 D.

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. With motion means what, and what do you add? Far point behind you (hyperopia or low myopia under the WD value); add plus.
  2. Against motion? Far point between you and the patient (myopia over the WD value); add minus.
  3. Neutral? Reflex fills the pupil with no movement; the far point is at your peephole (endpoint).
  4. Net finding formula? Net = gross minus working-distance power.
  5. Working-distance values for 67, 50, 40 cm? 1.50 D, 2.00 D, 2.50 D.
  6. Gross +2.50 at 50 cm, net? +0.50 D (2.50 - 2.00).
  7. Reflex near neutral? Fast, bright, and wide; far from neutral it is slow, dim, narrow.
  8. Dull, slow reflex in a dim room? Suggests high hyperopia (far from neutral).
  9. Streak tells for astigmatism? Break, skew, and width/thickness/speed differences between meridians.
  10. Sequence for astigmatism? Neutralize the first principal meridian with sphere, then the second (90 away) with cylinder.
  11. Static vs dynamic retinoscopy? Static = distance fixation, refractive error; dynamic (MEM/Nott) = near fixation, accommodative lag.
  12. MEM with motion means? A lag of accommodation; neutralize by adding plus (normal lag +0.25 to +0.75 D).
