Card03Subjective

Refine the retinoscopy: sphere, cylinder, balance

Subjective refraction takes the objective start and asks the patient to fine-tune it, on one rule that runs through every step: keep the least minus that still sees best, so accommodation never sneaks in. Learn the order, the cross cylinder, the duochrome, and the balance, and the chair sequence is automatic.

01 The order, and the one rule

Refine in a fixed sequence, each step assuming the last is set.

StepWhat you do
1Best sphere (MPMVA), fog and reduce to the least minus that gives best acuity
2Cylinder axis with the cross cylinder
3Cylinder power with the cross cylinder
4Re-check sphere (adding cyl shifted the focus)
5Binocular balance, then duochrome to finish
MPMVA, the maximum-plus rule

Give the most plus (least minus) that still reaches best acuity. Over-minus stimulates accommodation, so the patient reports 20/20 while quietly focusing, and you end up too strong. If more minus does not improve acuity, stop.

02 Fogging

Push the focus in front, then walk it back

Add plus to place the image in front of the retina, an artificial myopia that relaxes accommodation. Then reduce the plus in small steps until best acuity. Fogging is the reset that keeps the whole refraction honest.

03 Jackson cross cylinder

The JCC flips a small cylinder between two positions; the patient names the clearer, and you chase equality. Axis first, then power.

Axis

Hold the JCC with its axes 45 degrees to the correcting cylinder. Rotate the cylinder axis toward the preferred (clearer) position, in shrinking steps. Endpoint: no preference between flips means the axis is right.

Power (and keeping the blur centered)

Now align the JCC axis with the cylinder axis. If the flip that adds minus cyl is preferred, add minus cylinder in 0.25 steps until equal. For every −0.50 D of cyl added, add +0.25 D sphere to keep the circle of least confusion on the retina.

Hook

No preference on the flip = you are done with that setting. Chase equality, not a favorite.

04 Duochrome (red-green)

Chromatic aberration splits the focus: red focuses slightly behind the retina, green slightly in front. Balanced letters mean the focus sits between.

RAM-GAP, and a safety note

Red clearer → add minus (RAM). Green clearer → add plus (GAP). Start slightly fogged (so red leads) and reduce to equal. Because it works on focus and luminance, not hue, the duochrome is valid even in the color-deficient.

05 Binocular balancing

Equalize the accommodation between the two eyes

After the monocular refraction, balance so both eyes accommodate equally. Fog both, dissociate (prism or alternating occlusion), and add plus to the clearer eye until the two blur equally, then reduce together. It runs after monocular subjective, before binocular tests.

When it does not apply

Balancing needs roughly equal acuity in the two eyes. Skip it when one eye is much worse (dense unilateral cataract, amblyopia, no light perception), there is nothing to balance against.

06 Cross-links

Say these out loud

Card 2 (Retinoscopy): the objective result you refine here. Card 1 (Ametropia): the cross cylinder is finding the interval of Sturm and collapsing it. Physiological Optics: MPMVA, fogging, and balancing are all one idea, keep accommodation relaxed and equal.

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Refinement sequence? Sphere, cylinder axis, cylinder power, sphere again, then binocular balance (and duochrome).
  2. MPMVA principle? Give the most plus / least minus that still gives best acuity (avoids over-minus and accommodation).
  3. Purpose of fogging? Add plus to put the image in front of the retina and relax accommodation, then reduce.
  4. JCC axis refinement endpoint? Rotate the cyl axis toward the clearer flip; endpoint is no preference between the two positions.
  5. JCC power, and keeping the blur centered? Add minus cyl in 0.25 steps toward equality; add +0.25 sphere for every -0.50 cyl to keep the circle of least confusion on the retina.
  6. Duochrome: red clearer? Add minus (RAM: Red Add Minus).
  7. Duochrome: green clearer? Add plus / reduce minus (GAP: Green Add Plus).
  8. Is duochrome valid in color deficiency? Yes, it depends on focus and luminance, not hue.
  9. When is binocular balancing done? After monocular subjective refraction, before binocular testing.
  10. How do you balance? Fog and dissociate, add plus to the clearer eye until both blur equally, then reduce together.
  11. When do you skip balancing? When the eyes have very unequal acuity (dense cataract, amblyopia, no LP).
  12. Sphere refinement step size? 0.25 D.
