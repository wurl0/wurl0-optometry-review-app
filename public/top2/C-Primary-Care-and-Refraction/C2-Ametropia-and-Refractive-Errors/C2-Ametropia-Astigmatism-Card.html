Card01Ametropia

Every refractive error is a focus in the wrong place

Emmetropia lands parallel light on the retina with accommodation relaxed. Miss it three ways: too much power (myopia), too little (hyperopia), or two powers at once (astigmatism). Learn the far point, the interval of Sturm, and the transposition trick, and the classification questions become mechanical.

01 Emmetropia and the far point

With accommodation relaxed, the eye's far point is the one object distance that focuses sharply on the retina. Where that point sits names the error.

StateRelaxed focusFar pointCorrect with
EmmetropiaOn the retinaInfinityNothing
MyopiaIn front of retinaReal, in front (near)Minus (concave)
HyperopiaBehind retinaVirtual, behind the eyePlus (convex)
AstigmatismTwo focal lines (interval of Sturm)Two, one per meridianCylinder
Axial vs refractive

Each error is axial (eye too long or short) or refractive (cornea/lens too strong or weak). A myope's eye is usually too long; a hyperope's too short. About 1 mm of axial length is roughly 3 D.

02 Myopia and hyperopia, in kinds

Myopia types

Simple (physiologic), degenerative/pathologic (long eye, retinal risk), nocturnal (dim light, dark focus), pseudomyopia (accommodative spasm, disappears under cycloplegia), and induced/index (nuclear sclerosis, drugs). Corrected with the least minus that gives best acuity.

Hyperopia is layered

Total hyperopia = latent + manifest. Latent is hidden by ciliary tone, revealed only by cycloplegia. Manifest = facultative (accommodation can still overcome it) + absolute (it cannot). Give the most plus the patient accepts for comfortable vision.

Correction hook

Myopia: max minus is wrong, use the least (over-minus stimulates accommodation). Hyperopia: give the most plus that keeps distance clear.

03 Astigmatism and the interval of Sturm

A toric surface has two principal powers, so it makes not a point but two focal lines at right angles, with a blur between them.

Where the cylinder goes

A correcting cylinder collapses the two focal lines onto the retina. In minus-cylinder form, the axis marks the meridian with no cyl power; the full cyl acts 90 degrees away. Regular astigmatism has meridians at right angles (correctable); irregular (keratoconus, scars) does not, and needs a rigid contact lens.

04 Classifying astigmatism

By where the two focal lines fall

Read each meridian's power. Both minus = compound myopic. Both plus = compound hyperopic. One plano = simple (myopic or hyperopic, named by the other). One plus, one minus (they straddle the retina) = mixed.

By meridian orientation

With-the-rule (WTR): steeper vertical meridian, minus cyl axis near 180 (young eyes; the lids press the cornea). Against-the-rule (ATR): steeper horizontal, minus cyl axis near 90 (older eyes). Oblique: axis near 45 or 135.

Comparing the two eyes

Symmetric: the axes mirror each other (sum to about 180). Homonymous: both eyes the same type (both WTR or both ATR); heteronymous if one is WTR and the other ATR.

05 Transposition: same lens, other form

Three steps, every time

1. New sphere = old sphere + old cylinder. 2. New cylinder = same number, flip the sign. 3. New axis = old axis ± 90. The lens is identical; only the notation changes.

Worked example

+2.00 −1.00 × 90 transposes to +1.00 +1.00 × 180. Check the meridians both ways: at 90 the power is +1.00, at 180 it is +2.00, either notation gives the same two powers.

06 Cross-links

Say these out loud

Theoretical Optics: the interval of Sturm and transposition are pure lens optics. Primary Care Cards ahead: retinoscopy and the cross cylinder find and refine exactly this cylinder. Ocular Disease: irregular astigmatism flags keratoconus, and a sudden refractive shift flags cataract or blood-sugar swings.

07 Rebuild it on paper

1 · Far points

Draw where light focuses and where the far point sits for myope, hyperope, emmetrope.

2 · Hyperopia layers

Total = latent + manifest; manifest = facultative + absolute. Define each.

3 · Interval of Sturm

Draw the two focal lines and the circle of least confusion, label which meridian makes which line.

4 · Classify five prescriptions

Read the meridians and call each simple/compound/mixed, then WTR/ATR.

5 · Transpose

Transpose three prescriptions both directions and verify by meridian.

08 Spaced-repetition deck

Into the review queue, 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  1. Emmetropia? Parallel light focuses on the retina with accommodation relaxed (far point at infinity).
  2. Myopia: focus, far point, correction? Focuses in front of the retina; real far point in front of the eye; corrected with minus.
  3. Hyperopia: focus, far point, correction? Focuses behind the retina; virtual far point behind the eye; corrected with plus.
  4. Axial vs refractive, and the 1 mm rule? Eye too long/short vs optics too strong/weak; about 1 mm axial length is roughly 3 D.
  5. Total hyperopia components? Latent + manifest; manifest = facultative + absolute; latent needs cycloplegia to reveal.
  6. The interval of Sturm? The space between the two focal lines of a toric system; midway is the circle of least confusion.
  7. WTR vs ATR? WTR = steep vertical meridian (minus cyl axis ~180); ATR = steep horizontal (minus cyl axis ~90).
  8. Regular vs irregular astigmatism? Regular = principal meridians 90 apart (correctable); irregular (keratoconus, scars) needs a rigid lens.
  9. Classify by focal lines? Both minus = compound myopic; both plus = compound hyperopic; one plano = simple; straddling = mixed.
  10. Transposition steps? New sphere = sphere + cyl; flip the cyl sign; change axis by 90.
  11. Transpose +2.00 -1.00 x90? +1.00 +1.00 x180 (same lens).
  12. Symmetric vs homonymous astigmatism? Symmetric = mirror axes (sum ~180); homonymous = both eyes the same type (both WTR or both ATR).
