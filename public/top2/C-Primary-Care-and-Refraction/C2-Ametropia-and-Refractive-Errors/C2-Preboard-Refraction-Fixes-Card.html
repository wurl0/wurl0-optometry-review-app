Card07Refraction fixes

Primary care: refraction fixes for a strong subject

Primary Care was your best subject on the preboard (121/150). These are the few that slipped, and they cluster tightly: the logic of what makes an eye myopic or hyperopic, the myopia-control mechanisms, astigmatism classification, and retinoscopy. Two of the missed items were mis-keyed on the form, and this card corrects them so you do not learn the wrong thing.

01 Refractive error is power vs length

Every ametropia comes from two dials: the eye's refracting power and its axial length. Too much of either makes the focus fall short (myopia); too little makes it fall long (hyperopia). The "except" stems just ask which change does the opposite.

ChangeEffect
longer eyeballmyopia (axial)
steeper cornea = shorter radius of curvaturemyopia (more power)
shorter eyeball, normal powerhyperopia
flatter cornea = longer radiushyperopia (less power)
The "except" traps you missed

"Causes of hyperopia except" = shorter radius of curvature, because a shorter radius steepens the cornea, adds power, and causes myopia, not hyperopia. "Causes of myopia except" = shorter eyeball with normal power, because that is hyperopia. Read these by matching each option to power-or-length, then pick the one that lands on the wrong error.

Anchor

Shorter radius = steeper = stronger = myopia. Shorter eyeball = less distance = hyperopia. Radius and length pull opposite ways when you say "shorter."

02 Myopia control: it is all about defocus sign

Modern myopia control turns on where the peripheral image falls. Hyperopic defocus (image behind the retina) tells the eye to grow; myopic defocus (image in front) tells it to stop.

StemAnswer
peripheral hyperopic defocus in an animal model causesincreased axial elongation
historical rationale for undercorrectionmyopic defocus at the retina slows growth
atropine slows myopia mainly byretinal dopamine release modulation
myopia from nuclear sclerosis of the lensindex (induced) myopia
The two facts the board reuses

Atropine works through the retina (dopamine), not by relaxing the ciliary muscle. Undercorrection was believed to help by leaving myopic defocus, though the modern evidence went the other way. And a lens that goes cloudy with age (nuclear sclerosis) adds index, so it is index/induced myopia, not axial.

03 Astigmatism classification

Two labels, done in order: the rule (from the minus-cylinder axis), then the type (from where each meridian focuses).

Read thisThen call it
minus-cyl axis near 180 (or plus-cyl axis near 90)with-the-rule
minus-cyl axis near 90 (or plus-cyl axis near 180)against-the-rule
one meridian on the retina, the other myopicsimple myopic astigmatism
one meridian myopic, the other hyperopicmixed astigmatism
Worked example

−1.00 sph = +1.00 cyl x 180. Transpose to plano / −1.00 x 090. The minus-cyl axis is 90, so it is against-the-rule; one meridian is plano and the other is myopic, so simple myopic astigmatism. Answer: against-the-rule simple myopic astigmatism.

Careful with "symmetric / homologous"

The both-eyes labels (symmetric vs asymmetric, homologous vs heterologous) are defined differently across Philippine reviewers, so the preboard's keys here are source-dependent. Know the terms, but do not treat those two items as settled. Rule and type (above) are the standard, safe classification.

Mis-keyed on the form (do not learn it)

One item called a dull, slow "with" movement "high myopia." That is wrong: a "with" reflex means you still need plus, which is the hyperopic side; a dull, slow reflex means a large error, so the real answer is high hyperopia. High myopia gives an "against" reflex. Trust the with-plus / against-minus rule, not that key.

Sheard's logic

Exophoria is corrected by convergence, which is positive fusional vergence (base-out). Failing Sheard's means the reserve is too small for the phoria, so vision therapy builds the positive fusional reserve.

Congruous = posterior

The more congruous (matching) the two field defects, the more posterior the lesion. A congruous homonymous hemianopsia sits at the lateral geniculate body or occipital cortex, on the side opposite the field loss.

07 Spherical equivalent in one move

Adding cylinder shifts the circle of least confusion by half the cylinder. To keep it on the retina, move the sphere the opposite way by half.

The exact item

After adding −0.50 D of cylinder, add +0.25 D sphere to keep the interval of Sturm centered on the retina. Rule: sphere change = minus one-half of the cylinder change.

09 Spaced-repetition deck

Drop these into your FoqusLab review queue on the 1 / 2 / 3 / 7 / 14 day ladder. Answer out loud before you flip.

  2. Cause of myopia except? Shorter eyeball with normal power (that is hyperopia).
  3. Peripheral hyperopic defocus does what? Increases axial elongation.
  4. Atropine slows myopia by? Retinal dopamine release modulation.
  5. Nuclear sclerosis causes which myopia? Index (induced) myopia.
  6. Classify −1.00 = +1.00 x 180. Against-the-rule, simple myopic astigmatism.
  7. With vs against retinoscopy motion? With = add plus (hyperopic side); against = add minus (myopic side).
  8. Dull slow "with" in a dim room? High hyperopia (not high myopia; that form key was wrong).
  9. Purpose of dynamic retinoscopy? Measure refractive state during near fixation.
  10. Exophoria failing Sheard's: build what? Positive fusional reserve (base-out).
  11. Congruous homonymous hemianopsia lies where? Posterior pathway (LGB / occipital), opposite side.
  12. Sphere move after adding −0.50 cyl? Add +0.25 sphere (minus half the cylinder).
