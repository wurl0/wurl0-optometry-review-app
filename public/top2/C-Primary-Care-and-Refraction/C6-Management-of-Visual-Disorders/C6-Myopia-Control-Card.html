Card05Myopia control

Slow the growing eye with defocus

Myopia progresses because the eye keeps elongating, and the periphery of the retina votes on that growth. Feed it hyperopic defocus and it grows; feed it myopic defocus and it slows. Every control method, optical, drug, and behavioral, is a way to change that peripheral signal.

01 The problem is a long eye

Axial myopia is elongation of the globe, so the image falls in front of the retina. In children it progresses, and high myopia carries lifelong risk of retinal detachment, myopic maculopathy, and glaucoma. Control means slowing axial growth, not just sharpening today's vision.

Do not confuse these with progression

Pseudomyopia (accommodative spasm) reads as minus but is plus under cycloplegia. Index/induced myopia comes from nuclear sclerosis. Nocturnal myopia appears only in dim light. None are the axial elongation this card targets.

02 The engine: peripheral defocus

Why plain minus lenses do not help

A single-vision minus lens sharpens the fovea but leaves the curved peripheral retina in relative hyperopic defocus, a growth signal. Undercorrection was once thought to slow myopia (leftover myopic blur), but it actually speeds progression, so correct fully and change the periphery deliberately.

03 The interventions

OpticalDual-focus soft CL, DIMS/HAL spectacles, ortho-kImpose peripheral myopic defocus

Ortho-k and the reverse curve

The reverse-geometry lens flattens the central cornea (clear daytime vision) and steepens the mid-periphery. That reverse curve is the part that builds the peripheral myopic defocus ring.

Atropine's real mechanism

Its control effect is not from paralyzing accommodation, low doses barely touch focusing. It works on retinal dopamine and scleral receptors. Low dose is used to minimize glare and rebound.

04 Spotting and monitoring risk

Pre-myopia and risk factors

A child whose cycloplegic refraction is only about +0.75 D or less at age 6 to 7 is a pre-myope, at risk. Other risks: Asian ethnicity, low outdoor time, heavy near work, parental myopia, and early onset.

Track the eye, not just the glasses

Monitor axial length every 6 months. A crossing from plus into minus with rising axial length confirms onset and progression, prescription change alone can lag.

05 Combining methods

Additive combinations

Atropine + orthokeratology together outperform either alone (an additive effect). Optical and pharmacologic brakes hit different parts of the same signal, so stacking helps.
